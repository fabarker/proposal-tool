The template every slide is added from

The reference’s look is a template: the thin blue rules, the title and subtitle, the footnotes, the text and its styling. So the deck stops drawing its frame in code. The style lives in one PowerPoint file in the project, and every slide of a deck is added from that file’s one layout. Change the file and every deck follows.

How a deck is built from it

  1. Open the template, and leave every slide it holds as it is, but for the marks below.
  2. For each slide the deck plans, add a slide from Content at its marker (where the tool’s slides go); put the title, subtitle and footnotes in their placeholders; add the Proposal UID and the page number, which a slide added from a layout does not take on its own.
  3. Fit the slide’s tables, columns and boxes into the content box’s place, dressed from the levels, the samples and the theme’s roles.
  4. Fill the fields, draw the tables into their areas, take out the markers and the slides whose conditions do not apply; keep the template’s fonts rather than adding a second copy; lock the deck and mark it final, as now.

Changing it, or replacing it

  • In PowerPoint. Move or restyle the placeholders in Slide Master view, change the theme’s colours or fonts, restyle a sample. Keep the layout’s name, the placeholders’ indices and the samples’ names, and every deck follows.
  • With the house template. Poros uses its own as it is; the deck maps to it as described below, and the check says how before anything is installed.
  • From scratch. The builder made the first version. Once the file is edited by hand, the file is the source; running the builder again starts it over.

Where it lives, and Poros

The file in this project is a placeholder. Poros already has the house template, with the right title and subtitle formats in GS Sans, and keeps it. The placeholder never goes there: the Poros update carries the service package, the page and the router block, and deckTemplate/ is outside all three.

The deck reads whichever template the setting SCENARIO_DECK_TEMPLATE names: here the placeholder, on Poros the house template. There is no copy in the package, so an update can never carry a template over or replace the one Poros has.

How the deck uses whichever template it is given

  1. Text in a placeholder carries no formatting of its own. The title, subtitle, footnotes, UID and page number take their face, size, colour and place from the template, so the house template’s formats are what a delivered deck shows.
  2. What the deck draws names the template’s fonts and colours, not its own. Tables, boxes and rules ask for the theme’s body or heading font and for a theme colour, never for a face by name or a colour by value; on Poros they come out in the house template’s.
  3. The canvas and the content box are the template’s. Slide size and margins are read from the file, and every table is fitted into the box it finds.
  4. The template’s theme and fonts are left alone. Today the deck sets its own theme fonts and embeds its own copies of the faces; given a template, it does neither.
  5. Roles are found, not assumed. The deck finds the layout named in a second setting (Content unless told otherwise), and its placeholders by what they are: title, subtitle, body, footer, page number. A role the template does not have is left out rather than drawn by hand.
  6. A check before the update goes in. A read-only script, run on Poros against the house template, lists what the deck will use: the layout, each role and the placeholder it maps to, the canvas, the theme’s fonts and colours, and anything missing. Nothing is installed until it reads clean.

Your template’s own slides, and where the tool’s go

The house template may hold slides of its own that the tool must not touch, and the tool’s slides have to go in at set places among them: the asset allocation as slide 10, say, and the fee summary as slide 20, the numbers still to be decided.

Why not slide numbers. The tool’s slides are not a fixed set. A long table runs to a second slide, a proposal without fees has no fee slides, a custom portfolio has no risk slide, and any edit to the template moves every number after it. A number that is right for one proposal is wrong for the next. So the template marks the places instead, and the numbers fall out of where the marks sit.

Four marks, each made in PowerPoint

A mark goes on a slide, or on a shape or some text on a slide, never on the deck: the deck is the template file itself. The template’s README, deckTemplate/README.md, makes each one step by step.

The rules

  • A slide without a mark is not touched: its wording, pictures, charts, notes and transitions come out as they went in.
  • A marker whose slide does not apply is taken out: the fee slides of a proposal without fees, the risk slide of a custom portfolio.
  • A tool slide with no marker goes at the end, in the deck’s own order, and the check names it, so nothing is lost while the marks are being placed.
  • Nothing half-filled is delivered. A field the tool does not know, or a figure it cannot work out (a charge the PWA has not entered), stops the export with the slide and the field named, as the export’s other checks do. A delivered deck never shows {{…}}.
  • The tool takes its own tool: lines out of the notes of every delivered deck; a slide’s other notes stay.
  • Page numbers follow, because they are a field PowerPoint fills. A contents slide that types page numbers as text does not: it is the template’s, and left alone.
