Editing products and fees in the Repository

Add and change products from the product card, add and change fees from a new Fees tab, and keep a full record of who changed what and when.

Status proposal, awaiting your decisionsPrepared 16 October 2026Covers products · fees · the change log · moving Poros across

In short

Today products and fees are both delivered files the tool only reads. To edit them inside the tool they have to move into the tool's own database, the same one that already holds the sleeves, the overlay rules and the funding split. That database already records every sleeve change with the admin and the time. Products and fees would get the same treatment.

Products

An Edit button on the product card and a New product button on the Catalogue. Every product shows an As of date and who set it, plus its full history with a restore.

Fees

A new Fees tab in the Repository. Change rates in the grid, add a fee group, review the changes and save them with a note. The card's history can be restored the same way.

The log

Every change is kept: who (from the sign-in), when, what it was before and after, and the note. The Activity tab becomes the one place to see all of it. Each delivered proposal records which product and fee versions priced it.

The one real change of principle

The tool was built on the idea that the product file and the fee file are delivered by another team and never edited by hand. This plan reverses that: the database becomes the master, and a delivered file becomes something an admin imports, with a preview of what it would change. If a data team still sends files, nothing is lost: an import is reviewed and logged like any other change.

For comparison, sleeves already have everything this plan asks for: each save is a new revision recording the admin and the time, nothing is ever deleted, an earlier version can be restored, and a save made against an out-of-date screen is refused. The overlay rules and the funding split are kept the same way. The plan reuses that machinery rather than inventing a second one.

How it will behave

  • One database. Products and the fee card go into sleeves.db, the Repository's database. Sleeves are checked against products at save time, and having both in one file keeps that check in a single step. It also means one file to back up.
  • Every change is a revision. A save adds a row recording the whole product (or the whole fee card) as it stood afterwards. Earlier rows are never rewritten or removed, so any earlier version can be read back and put back in force.
  • Who and when come from the sign-in. The admin's sign-in name is taken from the session, never typed. The time is the server's clock, the same as sleeves today.
  • Nothing is destroyed. A product is withdrawn, not deleted, and it can be restored. A fee group is removed only when no product uses it, and the removal stays in the history.
  • The same checks as the file. Everything the product and fee files are checked for today is checked at save, naming the field, so a bad value is refused while you're looking at it.
  • Two admins at once. If someone else saved the same product or the fee card after you opened it, your save is refused with a message, and the screen reloads their version. Nothing is silently overwritten.
  • Takes effect at once. A change reaches proposals being built straight away: no restart and no refresh of the file. A proposal already delivered keeps its workbook and deck exactly as they were sent, and its record says which product and fee versions priced it.

The As of date

Each product's As of is the moment its current values took effect, with the admin who saved them. A product loaded from the file on day one shows the load date and seeded from file. The date shows up in four places:

  • The product card, under the name: As of 16 Oct 2026, 14:02 · pmgadmin · version 3.
  • A new Catalogue column, As of. It can be sorted, and it can be filtered with a facet: Changed in the last 7 / 30 / 90 days.
  • The Catalogue header. Catalogue as of becomes the date of the most recent change to any product, not the file's date.
  • The Activity tab, as one entry per change (see the change log).
A second date, if you want it

The stamp above records when the tool was told. If you also need when the figures are true (for example, yields as of the last month-end), the product can carry a Data as of date. The admin types it, and it's checked to be a real date no later than today. That's decision 2 below.

Changing a product: the steps

  1. Open the Catalogue, then open the product's card.
  2. Click Edit. The card turns into a form with the current values filled in. The Product ID is shown but locked.
  3. Change what's needed. Each field is checked as you leave it, and a problem shows under that field.
  4. Above the Save button, the card shows what the change will reach. For example: This product is in 3 sleeves across 2 implementation types. Proposals being built that use them are re-priced at once.
  5. If the minimum investment goes up, the card adds: A position below a product's minimum stops the download. 2 sleeves give this product less than the new minimum on a $10m mandate.
  6. Optionally add a note (up to 240 characters), then click Save. The card goes back to view mode with the new As of date, and a new History row.

Adding a product

  1. On the Catalogue, click + New product beside the search box.
  2. Choose Start from blank, or Copy an existing product, which fills everything except the ID and the name. This is the same choice the New sleeve form already offers.
  3. Type the name. An ID is suggested from it, and you can change the ID until you save.
  4. Fill in the fields and click Save. The product shows up in the Catalogue straight away, as version 1.
  5. A new product isn't in any sleeve yet. The card says Not in any sleeve yet and offers Add to a sleeve →, which opens the Sleeves tab.

Withdrawing and restoring

  • Withdraw, on the card's menu, takes a product out of the Catalogue. It's refused while any live sleeve holds the product, and the message lists those sleeves with links so you can take it out of them first. That's decision 6 below.
  • Withdrawn products stay visible under a Show withdrawn filter on the Catalogue, with who withdrew them and when. Restore brings one back.
  • Withdrawing never removes history, and a withdrawn product's ID can't be reused.

Loading a product file: the re-seed route

The file is no longer read live. Instead, the Catalogue gets an Import file button, and the sleeve tool gets a matching command. An import always shows a preview first:

  1. Choose the file. It's checked exactly as the file is checked today. A file with any error is refused whole, naming the row and the field, and nothing is changed.
  2. Read the preview. Products in the catalogue but missing from the file are kept unless you tick them to be withdrawn, and the same rule as above applies to those that are in sleeves.
  3. Click Apply import. Each new or changed product gets a revision marked imported with your name, the file's name and the time. Unchanged products aren't touched, so their As of dates stay true.

This is the route for the Poros re-seed you're planning: import the new file and review the preview, and the log then records the re-seed as one dated event. The re-seeding guide would be rewritten around it.

Changing rates: the steps

  1. Repository → Fees → Edit rates.
  2. Pick the view (by fee group or by tier) and type the new rates. Any number of rates, across any groups and tiers, go into one change.
  3. Click Review and save. A summary lists every rate that moves, e.g. RDR · Core Active · $10m – $25m · PMG Target: 0.44% → 0.46%, and what it reaches, e.g. 19 products in the Core Active group.
  4. Type a note, which is required for fee changes (decision 5), e.g. 2027 pricing round, approved by the pricing committee 14 Oct.
  5. Click Save. The card becomes version 5 with your name and the time, and proposals being priced use it from the next calculation.

History, restore and import

  • History lists every version of the card: who, when, the note, and each rate that moved. Any earlier version has Restore, which saves it again as a new version, so the restore is logged too.
  • Import file takes a fee file in today's format and shows the same rate-by-rate preview (the fee tool's diff already does this). Applying it saves a version marked imported, with the file's name. The fee tool's command does the same from the terminal.

What a fee change reaches

  • Proposals being built: re-priced at once, on their next calculation.
  • Custom rates on a proposal keep their entered value. The floor and ceiling they must sit between come from the card, so they move with it. Today a custom rate is checked against them only when it is entered, so the plan adds a check that flags one now outside them before the proposal can be downloaded.
  • Delivered proposals: the workbook and the deck already sent are never changed. The register records the fee card version that priced each one.
  • The workbook's Fee Card line, which today prints the file's version, prints Version 5 · as of 16 Oct 2026.

The change log

Every change to products, fees, sleeves, overlay rules and the funding split is written to the same database as a permanent record:

What's recordedWhere it comes from
WhoThe signed-in admin's name, from the session
WhenThe server's clock, to the second
WhatThe action: created, updated, imported, withdrawn, restored, reverted, seeded
Before and afterThe full record after the change. The record before it is the previous version, so field-by-field differences are always available.
WhyThe note, if one was given (required for fees)

One Activity tab for everything

The Activity tab shows sleeve changes only today. It gains an Area filter: Sleeves · Products · Fees · Overlay rules · Funding split. Its existing filters (action, admin, date range, search) and its CSV download then work across all of them. An entry reads, for example:

16 Oct 2026 14:02  Products  updated   pmgadmin  GS Access IG Corporate ETF       Product cost 0.12 → 0.14   "Repriced by the issuer"
16 Oct 2026 11:30  Fees      updated   pmgadmin  Fee card v5                     2 rates moved              "2027 pricing round…"
15 Oct 2026 17:05  Sleeves   updated   pmgadmin  Core Fixed Income (PMG ESG)     2 products reweighted

What a delivered proposal records

The register gains two facts for each proposal delivered from then on: the product catalogue version and the fee card version it was priced with. That answers the question what were the fees when this went out? from the register alone, and the version can be opened from the History.

What changes underneath

For the record. You don't need to read this to make the decisions.

  • Database. The Repository's database moves to its next schema version. It gains a products table with a product history table. The fee card is stored like the overlay rules: the whole card per version, about 180 rates.
  • Products in the service. The rest of the tool keeps asking for products exactly as now and gets the same shape back, now read from the database. The file reader stays, used for the first load and for imports.
  • Fees in the service. The card is read from the database and refreshed when a new version is saved, so no restart is needed. The check that the card is complete and consistent moves from service start to save time, so a broken card can't be saved in the first place.
  • New admin-only service calls: create, change, withdraw, restore and revert a product; a product's history; import preview and apply; read and save the fee card against the version it was opened at; its history; restore; fee import preview and apply.
  • Terminal tools. The sleeve tool gains product export, import and history. The fee tool's import writes a new version into the database instead of replacing the file.
  • Templates. The data template's products and feeRates sheets stay as the format for the first load and for imports.

Moving Poros across

  1. First, the share class update on its own. Poros's product file already uses the full words, which Poros's current code refuses. That change is ready and waiting, so it should go to Poros before any of this starts.
  2. The update that brings this work runs like the account card one: stop isgPMGService, copy the file, run go, start the services. During go, after the usual backup, the database is upgraded:
    • the products are loaded once from the file Poros uses today, each marked seeded from file with the file's name;
    • the fee card is loaded once from Poros's fee file, as version 1 marked seeded;
    • nothing else changes: the sleeves, the register and the overlay rules are kept as they are.
  3. After the update, changing the product file on Poros does nothing by itself. A new file goes in through Import file, so that's the moment to do your re-seed, and the log records it.
  4. Undo restores the database exactly as it was before, as with every update so far. That gets tested on the fake Poros before you're given the update.

How it gets tested

  • Products: create, change, withdraw, restore and revert, each leaving the right revision; every field check refusing with the right message; the refusal of a save made against an out-of-date screen; a withdraw refused while a sleeve holds the product; import preview and apply, and an import with one bad row changing nothing.
  • Fees: a rate change re-pricing a proposal on its next calculation with no restart; the band check; adding a group with every rate and removing one refused while in use; restore; an import matching today's fee-tool diff exactly.
  • The first load: a database of today's shape upgrades, and the products and fees it serves afterwards are identical, field for field and rate for rate, to what the files served before. Every existing workbook and deck test passes unchanged.
  • The log: every action appearing in Activity under the right area, admin and time, and in the CSV download.
  • Access: every new service call refused for anyone who isn't an admin.
  • Poros: the update on the fake Poros with its current files, then undo restoring the database exactly.

Decisions for you

My recommendation comes first in each. The plan above is written as if every recommendation is accepted.

1 · Which is the master for products?

The database (recommended), with the file as an import. Or: keep the file as the master and store the edits made in the tool as overrides on top of it. Overrides are harder to explain, and harder to reason about when the next file arrives.

2 · The As of date

The automatic stamp only (recommended to start): when the values took effect and who saved them. Or add a typed Data as of date for when the figures were sourced.

3 · How much of the fee card can be edited at first?

Rates, and adding and removing fee groups (recommended); renaming groups and editing tiers in a second release. Or all of it at once.

4 · Who may change fees?

The same admins as the rest of the Repository (recommended to start), with every change logged. Or a separate, shorter list of fee admins, which would need a new setting on Poros.

5 · Notes on saving

Required for fee changes, optional for products (recommended). Or optional everywhere, or required everywhere.

6 · Withdrawing a product that's in a sleeve

Refused, listing the sleeves (recommended). Or allowed, which leaves those sleeves broken and held back from proposals until fixed, as happens today when a product goes missing from the file.

7 · Should the As of date appear in the workbook or deck?

No: on screen only (recommended); the register records the versions. Or add a product As of column to the workbook's Implementation sheet.

8 · What the Activity tab covers

Everything, including overlay rules and the funding split (recommended), so there's one log. Or only products and fees beside sleeves; the other two already have histories in their own tabs.

9 · Updates to Poros

Two (recommended): products after stage 2, fees and the log after stage 4. Or one at the end.

10 · The share class change

Push it and send it to Poros now, on its own (recommended), because Poros's product file already needs it.

Things to be aware of

  • Mistakes are easier to make. Today a wrong rate needs someone to edit a file. Afterwards any admin can make one in seconds. The review step, the required note for fees, the history and one-click restore are the guard rails, and every change can be traced to a person.
  • The file stops being the truth. Anyone used to opening the product file to check a value would need to look in the tool instead, or use the export.
  • Other people's records. If the fees are also recorded somewhere official outside the tool, changes made here won't flow back there. The note and the export are the bridge.
  • The guides. The re-seeding guide and the admin manual both describe the files as read only. Both get updated in stage 5.

Plan only: no code, data or database has been changed. Once you've answered the decisions, stage 1 can start.
