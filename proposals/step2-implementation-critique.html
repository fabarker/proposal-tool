Part oneReading the screen

The rail carries a sleeve picker per category, two overlay tick boxes and the whole pricing block. The document carries a fifteen-column model table, five composition doughnuts, and an export card. It is the second of two steps and it ends in a file.

Who

The same PWA or analyst who built the allocation in step 1 — now turning it into something that can actually be bought and quoted.

The task

Attach a sleeve to every category, settle the overlays and the price, clear every blocker, download the workbook. The Excel file is the deliverable; this screen exists to produce it.

What makes it different from step 1

Step 1 asks for a judgment — which portfolio. Step 2 asks for completion: fill every slot, satisfy every constraint, ship. So its two jobs are showing progress and clearing blockers.

It does the first job three times over and the second one badly. Nearly every recommendation below moves weight from decoration onto the blocker, and onto the figures a PWA is here to read.

Part twoWhere the width goes

Measured on the live page at a 1,230px document width — a 15" laptop with the rail open. The table asks for 1,983px, so 753px is off-screen, and the part that is off-screen is the part with the money in it.

A PWA opens step 2 and sees categories, products, allocation, and seven attributes. Product cost, management fee, weighted fee, minimum investment and notional are all past the right edge. The five columns that decide whether this proposal is any good are the five you cannot see without scrolling sideways — and the header scrolls away vertically at the same time.

Part threeWhere the friction is

  1. The money is off-screen and the header is gone. 753px hidden to the right; and .tbl.impl thead th is position:sticky;top:0 inside an overflow-x:auto wrapper, which makes that wrapper the scrollport — so it never sticks. Twenty rows, 613px tall: by the Hedge Funds band you have neither column names nor figures.
  2. A blocked export names three remedies and one of them does not exist. “Raise the mandate, change the sleeve, or drop the products.” There is no control anywhere on this page that drops a product from a sleeve. A sleeve is atomic.
  3. The blocker is stated 753px away from the rows it names. Four rows tint pink; the below mandate minimum badge sits in the Notional cell, off-screen. At rest, the breach is communicated by background colour alone.
  4. Nothing says whether the remedy is realistic. The four breaches here would clear at mandates of $40.9m, $52.6m, $75.9m and $92.1m against a $10m mandate. “Raise the mandate” is not the answer and the screen could say so — it has every number it needs.
  5. “Total” is not a total. With only the auto overlays attached, a bold navy band reads Total 10.20%. That looks like a portfolio that does not add up, not like “10.2% implemented so far”.
  6. Pricing lives below the fold of a 1,552px rail in a 928px window. Include Fees, schedule, level, the custom rates and their editor are all past the four sleeve pickers. Changing a fee level means scrolling the rail every time.
  7. The fee card opens on the wrong schedule. The rail prices this proposal on RDR; View fee card opens with its own selector on CASP · one rate for every product, the first entry in its list, with nothing to say it is not what you are paying.
  8. Progress is announced three times and none of them mentions the blocker. A rail count, a rail bar with a sentence, and a header count with a bar — while the one fact that matters (four positions cannot be bought) appears only at the very bottom of the page.

A · The model tableBring the figures back on screen

A1

Fold Ticker, Style, Vehicle, Share class, Source, Liquidity and Exposure under the product name

High impactM
Element
IMPL_TEXT_COLUMNS and the seven <td class="txt"> cells per row in renderView(); .tbl.impl{min-width:1690px}.
Change
One specification line under the name, in a fixed order: ticker · class · vehicle · style · share · source · exposure · liquidity. Fifteen columns become eight; the table needs about 1,320px instead of 1,983px, which fits at today's width with the fee columns on. Rows go from 26px to about 44px.
Why
Those seven columns cost 660px — a third of the table — to carry attributes that almost never decide anything on their own, and they are what pushes cost, fee, minimum and notional past the fold. The argument is stronger here than on the allocation table, because this page already summarises those exact seven fields in the five doughnuts beneath it. The table is carrying them per row and in aggregate; only one of those needs to be a column.
Tradeoff
You lose the ability to run your eye down the vehicle column. The doughnuts answer that in aggregate and the workbook keeps the columns, so what is lost is per-row scanning of a secondary attribute. Rows get taller: about 14 on screen instead of 20. I would take that trade — a shorter table you can read beats a longer one you cannot.

A2

Move Notional next to Allocation

S
Element
Column order in renderView()'s <thead> and row builders.
Change
Categories · Products · Allocation · Notional · cost · fees · minimum.
Why
Allocation % and Notional are one fact in two units, and they are currently at opposite ends of a 1,983px table — Notional is the widest column at 277px and sits last. Put beside its percentage, the money reads without a journey, and notional against minimum — the comparison the whole breach rule turns on — becomes two adjacent columns rather than one at each end.
Tradeoff
The workbook's sheet has its own order. I would let the screen differ: this is the same screen-versus-report split already recorded for other surfaces.
A3

Dock this table's column headers

DefectHigh impactS
Element
.tbl.impl thead th{position:sticky;top:0} inside .tblwrap{overflow-x:auto}; DOCK_TABLES in core.js.
Change
The sticky rule is inert for the same reason it was on the allocation and risk tables — the horizontally scrolling wrapper becomes the sticky scrollport, and it never scrolls vertically. The docked-header mechanism already exists and lists only ['alloc','risk']. Add the implementation table, and give it the same right-edge shadow.
Why
Twenty rows over 613px with fifteen columns, and by the time you are at Hedge Funds the header is gone. It is worse here than in step 1 because the columns are less guessable: Prod cost, Mgmt fee and Wtd fee are three adjacent numbers in three different units.
Tradeoff
The dock draws from the live header cells, so folding the columns (A1) changes what it draws without any extra work. Do A1 first if both are planned.
A4

Give the category band its own figures instead of six empty cells

S
Element
tr.cat in renderView(): <td colspan="implBandSpan()"></td> plus three more empty cells.
Change
Put the band's own roll-up where the columns already are: weighted product cost, the sleeve's blended management fee, the count of products, and the notional it already shows. It carries Wtd fee and Notional today and nothing else.
Why
Six categories each spend a full row saying almost nothing, in a table that is short of width and tall with rows. “What does this category cost me” is the question a band is best placed to answer, and it is the unit a PWA actually trades — they swap a sleeve, not a product.

B · BlockersThe one thing standing between this screen and its file

B1

Say what the mandate would have to be, and offer only remedies that exist

High impactDefectM
Element
The breached branch in renderView() and the .export-gate copy.
Change
For each breach the page already holds the weight and the minimum, so the mandate that would clear it is minimum ÷ weight. State the largest, name the categories to change, and give two buttons: Edit the mandate (opens the mandate dialog) and Change the sleeve (focuses that category's picker). Drop “or drop the products”.
Why
On the scenario I measured, the four breaches clear at $40.9m, $52.6m, $75.9m and $92.1m against a $10m mandate. A PWA reading “raise the mandate” has no way to know they would need to multiply it by nine — so they try it, fail, and try again. One computed number turns a guessing game into a decision: this sleeve cannot be held at this size; change the sleeve. And “drop the products” should not be offered at all, because nothing on this page can do it.
Tradeoff
The ÷weight figure assumes the weight stays put as the mandate grows, which is true here — weights are fractions of the mandate. Word it as “about”, and it is honest.

B2

Put the breach chip beside the product name, in the pinned column

High impactS
Element
The item.belowMinimum badge, currently appended inside the Notional cell; tr.below-min's pink fill.
Change
A short below min chip after the product name in the .prodcol cell, which is pinned and therefore always on screen. Keep the figures in the Notional cell where they belong; shorten the long badge to a tooltip there.
Why
At rest the reader sees four pink rows and no reason — the explanation is 753px to the right. Even after A1 brings the money into view, the chip belongs next to the thing it is about. It also removes a WCAG 1.4.1 problem: right now, in the default viewport, the only carrier of that information is a background colour.
B3

Make the named products in the export card jump to their rows

S
Element
The breach list in the gate text; the “and 1 more” truncation.
Change
Render each name as a button that scrolls its row into view and flashes it. Drop the truncation — with four items there is no reason to hide the fourth, and hiding it means the one product the reader cannot see is the one they cannot go and look at.
Why
The card is at the bottom of a long page and names rows several screens up. Today the reader has to scroll back and find them by eye among twenty.
B4

Surface the blocker under the table, not only at the foot of the page

M
Element
New strip between the table and .impl-viz; the existing .export-gate text.
Change
A single line directly under the model: “Ready to download”, or the blocker in one sentence with the same two buttons. The export card keeps its full copy.
Why
The reader's attention is on the table — that is where they just changed something. The consequence of that change is currently below five doughnut cards and a horizontal rule. A status line where the work happens closes the loop; the card at the foot is then a confirmation rather than a discovery.

C · The railA 1,552px scroll in a 928px window

C1

Collapse the sleeve list once it is complete, so pricing is reachable

High impactM
Element
#tier-sleeves: four pickers, two overlays, then the whole .fee-group.
Change
When every category has a sleeve, roll the picker list into a summary — “4 of 4 · SMA Only, Mutual Funds, Passive, HFM & ART” with an Edit, the pattern the base tier already uses. Pricing then sits on the first screen of the rail instead of the second.
Why
The rail is 1,552px in a 928px viewport. Everything about price — the toggle, the card, the schedule, the level, five custom rates and their editor — is past the fold, behind four selects that are finished. Pricing is the part a PWA revisits; sleeves are the part they set once.
Tradeoff
One click to change a sleeve later. Worth it: the sleeve is also changeable from the table's pill (C3), and the overlays stay visible.
C2

Show what a sleeve costs and holds, in the picker

High impactM
Element
.sl-row in renderRail(); the sleeve library entries already carry their products.
Change
Under each select, a line for the sleeve now chosen: product count, weighted all-in cost, and a flag when any product's minimum is above what this category's weight would buy. The same figures in the option labels where they fit.
Why
Choosing between SMA Only, Mutual Funds and Passive is the central act of this screen, and it is currently made blind: the consequence only appears after the choice, in a table whose cost columns are off-screen. Every number needed is already client-side — managementFee() and the products' own costs are what the table's own wtdBp is built from.
Tradeoff
A cost shown before the model is rebuilt is an estimate at that category's current weight; say “at this weight”. Still far better than nothing.

C3

Make the table's sleeve pill change the sleeve, not only remove it

S
Element
.pill.p-sleeve with its data-rmsleeve ×, in each tr.cat.
Change
Make the pill itself a button that focuses and opens that category's picker in the rail. Keep the × for removal.
Why
The table is where a PWA sees that a sleeve is wrong; the only thing they can do there is delete it, which empties the category and blocks the export. The natural action — swap it — sends them to the rail to find the right select among four. One of the two things you can do to a sleeve is available in both places; the other, in neither.

D · Progress & totalsSay one thing once, and say the right thing

D1

Label the total row for what it is

High impactS
Element
tr.grand: <th>Total</th> and t.weight.
Change
Call it Implemented total, and while the model is incomplete print the weight as “10.20% of 100%” with the remainder named beside it — “89.80% not yet implemented”.
Why
On a fresh step 2 with only the auto overlays attached, a heavy navy band reads Total 10.20%. Every other total in this product sums to 100, so this one reads as a portfolio that does not add up — the most alarming possible way to say “you have not started yet”. It is also the first thing a new user sees on this screen.
D2

One progress statement, and let it carry the blocker

S
Element
.completion-summary in the document head; .tier-count and .sl-progress in the rail.
Change
Keep the rail's count beside the Sleeves heading and the bar under the list; drop the sentence that repeats them. In the document head, replace the count with the state that actually gates the download: “4 of 4 sleeves · 4 positions below minimum”, in the gate's own colour.
Why
Three progress indicators say the same easy thing, and the hard thing is said once, at the bottom. When everything is attached, all three read 4 of 4 and the screen looks finished — while the export is blocked.

E · The doughnutsFive cards of equal weight, unequal value

E1

Collapse a single-slice doughnut to a sentence

S
Element
renderDonuts() / DONUT_DIMENSIONS.
Change
When a dimension has one value, draw no ring: print “Source — all Internal” in the card. With two or more, keep the doughnut.
Why
On the measured model, Source is 100% Internal: a full, confident ring carrying one bit of information, given the same 196px and the same visual weight as Vehicle's genuine three-way split. On a fresh step 2 — overlays only — all five are single-value rings reading 100%, which is the least informative the block can be at the exact moment it is most prominent.
E2

Fold the block until the model is complete

S
Element
The .impl-viz section and its done flag, which today only changes the note.
Change
While incomplete, collapse it to its heading and the note that already exists (“across the 10.20% attached so far”), open on click. Expanded by default once complete.
Why
Composition of a fifth of a portfolio is not a useful read, and the block sits between the table and the export card — so while the model is unfinished it pushes the deliverable, and the reason it is blocked, further off screen. The honest note under the heading is an admission that the charts are premature; act on it.
Tradeoff
Some will want the composition mid-build to sanity-check a sleeve. A disclosure keeps that one click away rather than removing it.

G · AccessibilitySpecific gaps

G1

A breach must not be colour alone in the default viewport

DefectS
Element
.tbl.impl tr.below-min (a #FDE8E8 fill) and the badge inside the Notional cell, 753px to the right.
Change
B2 fixes it — the chip beside the pinned product name. Until then, at minimum give the row a left edge rule and a <th>-side marker.
Why
As it stands, without scrolling sideways, “this position cannot be bought” is carried by a pale pink background and nothing else. WCAG 1.4.1, and the pink is subtle enough to be missed on a dim projector in a meeting room.
G2

Replace the padlock emoji on auto-attached sleeves

S
Element
The ' 🔒' appended to the sleeve pill for an auto category, with no label.
Change
An inline SVG with role="img" and a label — “attached by rule” — or simply the words. Give the pill a title saying why it cannot be changed.
Why
Emoji are announced inconsistently across screen readers and platforms: “locked”, “lock”, the raw codepoint, or nothing. It is also the only explanation on screen for why two categories have no picker in the rail — worth spelling out for everyone, not just for assistive tech.
G3

Announce the model's state when it changes, not just its parts

S
Element
The live regions in core.js; the gate text in renderView().
Change
On attaching or removing a sleeve, announce the consequence as well as the act: “Passive attached to Public Equity. 4 of 4 categories. Export blocked: 4 positions below minimum.”
Why
A sighted user learns the export is blocked by seeing the card change colour at the foot of the page. A screen-reader user has no equivalent, and the gate text is only read when focus reaches the download button — by which point the reason is several hundred words away from the action that caused it.

H · ResponsiveTwo pinned columns take half the screen

H1

Unpin the Products column below the stacking breakpoint

M
Element
.tbl.impl .prodcol{position:sticky;left:var(--impl-c1,248px)} and publishPinnedColumnWidth().
Change
Under about 1,000px, pin only the first column and cap it; let Products scroll with the rest. Cap the first column the way the allocation table now does and let its labels wrap.
Why
At an 840px document width the two pinned columns measure 449px — 53% of everything visible — while 1,143px of the table is hidden. Two frozen columns and a 391px window onto thirteen more is not a table you can read; it is two labels and a peephole.
H2

Reconsider keeping the whole rail on step 2 when narrow

M
Element
The narrow rail summary, which deliberately applies to step 1 only — “step 2 keeps the whole rail: its pickers are the work there”.
Change
Apply it once every sleeve is attached, with the summary line reading the sleeve count and the fee level. Before that, keep the full rail — the reasoning for step 1 holds while there is still something to pick.
Why
At 896px the rail stacks above the document at its full 1,552px, so a finished set of controls occupies nearly two screens before the model begins. The condition that justified the exception — that the pickers are the work — stops being true the moment they are all answered, which is exactly when C1 would have collapsed them anyway.
Tradeoff
Pricing then lives behind the summary's Edit on a tablet. Acceptable if F2 puts the resulting fee in the summary line, so the number is visible even when the control is not.

FinallyWhat to leave alone

This screen gets several hard things right. None of the changes above should cost them.

  • The export gate's three voices — working, blocked, failed — wired to the button through aria-describedby, with the button disabled rather than hidden. The wording is the problem, not the mechanism.
  • The breach is a hard block, enforced on the server too. A position below its minimum cannot be bought, so the materials cannot be produced. Exactly right, and worth the friction.
  • Weights round to exactly 100.00 by largest remainder, and rows that print as zero are dropped after the rounding so the column still closes. The sum can be checked by hand, which is the whole point of a deliverable.
  • The doughnuts take colour from the value's name, not the slice's position — the same rule the category charts follow, so Passive keeps its hue when it overtakes Active.
  • Shares are of what is attached, and the note says so. An unfinished model is not allowed to imply it is finished. E2 acts on that honesty rather than contradicting it.
  • The fee card is read-only and says so, and its tier row is marked this mandate. The card is a delivery; nothing here should be able to edit it.
  • The fee layer's reveal — rail block and table columns animating together in one pass, with a real leave animation before the cells are destroyed. Fiddly, and it works.
