Ten strips along the foot

Option 3 from the entry-point review, taken ten ways. Same five destinations, same admin-only gate, same place on the page — ten different answers to how loud it should be and how much it should say.

Each is rendered below the real landing hero, at the real type and colour, carrying the live figures: 101 sleeves, 73 products, 14 proposals, 104 versions on record.

FirstWhat is fixed across all ten

These are ten treatments of one decision that has already been made. Everything below varies only in weight, shape and how much it says.

Five destinations

Sleeves, Catalogue, Archive, Activity, Proposals — each deep-linking to #repository, #catalogue, #archive, #activity, #proposals, which already restore their own filters.

One gate

Rendered only when capabilities.canAdmin is true. A non-admin sees today’s page with nothing missing — no gap, no placeholder, no empty bordered strip.

One position

Below the hero, at the foot of the landing content. Only variant 7 leaves the flow, and it says so.

Two things to hold in mind while comparing. The landing page is client-facing — a PWA may have it open with someone looking over their shoulder, so admin furniture should not announce itself. And the counts are free: the schema the landing already fetches carries the store’s totals, so any variant that shows numbers costs no extra call.

Design 1Hairline rule

1

A rule, a label, five words

Quietest

The least a strip can be and still be a strip. One hairline, aligned to the hero’s own measure, and the five names as plain links. It reads as part of the document rather than as a bar bolted to it.

3

Chips, with the counts — and a last-change line if the call is cheap

Recommended

The chips are the only variant that is both unmistakably clickable and quiet enough for a client-facing page, and they speak a control language the admin has already met twice inside the console. Why this one

  • Obviously clickable without being loud — the failure of 1 and 5, avoided without the cost of 2 and 4.
  • Already a pattern in this product, so nothing new to learn or to style.
  • Wraps cleanly at phone width with no special case.

Two decisions it needs

  • Zero reads as a dash. Archive 0 advertises an empty room; an em dash says “nothing here” without making it a headline.
  • The last-change line is optional. It is the best idea in the ten, but it needs the newest activity entry on the landing page. If that call is not cheap, ship the chips and add the line later — they are independent.
