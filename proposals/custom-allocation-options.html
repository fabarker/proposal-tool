Revision 2: a custom portfolio of its own

You suggested a different entrance. Custom becomes a choice in the rail's Allocation selector, and choosing it opens Implementation in the whole-portfolio view, as a blank slate. There is no "stood at" and no "By sleeve", so the rail looks different for a custom proposal. I think this is the right shape, and better than option 6 as I first described it. It turns "custom" from a variation of a strategic proposal into a kind of proposal of its own, with its own clear journey. I would make four adjustments, all described below. Option 6 is rewritten as this path, with a working walk-through.

Why it works

Two kinds of proposal

A strategic proposal chooses an analysed portfolio and implements it with sleeves. A custom proposal is built from nothing. Nobody has to manage a starting point they never chose.

Adjust

Four changes to the idea

Keep the risk level, as a risk profile. Make the rail follow the kind of proposal, not the route in. Turn sleeves into building blocks. Give the allocation step a custom column.

Cost

The largest change of any option

Today every part of the tool assumes the proposal's portfolio is a strategic one, and a custom portfolio is a new kind of portfolio everywhere. No risk figures can be produced for it.

Why it works

  1. The decision is made where it belongs. Choosing the allocation is the allocation step's job, and "build it myself" is an allocation choice. The PWA does not reach Implementation, then find a switch that changes the allocation after all.
  2. No baseline to explain. Without a starting point there is no "stood at", no points moved and no approval caused by drifting from something the PWA never intended to keep. The controls describe the portfolio itself, not its distance from another one.
  3. The rail shows only what applies. A custom portfolio has no sleeves to choose and no overlays to switch on, so neither is shown. Pricing still is.
  4. The two paths stop interfering. Custom sleeves stay what they are: small changes inside a strategic proposal. Reshaping the allocation happens in its own path, which is what makes % of the portfolio (option 2) unnecessary.
  5. It matches how bespoke mandates are actually built: a PM starts from the products and the client's constraints, not from a model portfolio.

Four adjustments

  1. Keep the risk level, as a risk profile. A blank slate still needs limits, and the client's risk profile is the natural source. It sets each category's allowed range (Moderate: equity 30–46% before approval, 23–53% at most). It fills in the account opening form's risk level. It also gives the files a labelled reference if you want one. It is not a starting point, so nothing is filled in from it.
  2. The rail follows the kind of proposal, not the route in. A custom proposal shows the custom rail whether it was reloaded tomorrow, opened from the register, or reached through the step tabs. The route only decides which step you land on first.
  3. Sleeves become building blocks, not a mode. "Insert a sleeve" puts a library sleeve's products into the build at a weight you choose, and from then on they are ordinary rows. Most custom portfolios will be assembled from familiar pieces, and this keeps the library useful without bringing back "By sleeve".
  4. The allocation step keeps a job. The allocation table shows the custom portfolio as its own column, built from its products: category rows, with the products' asset classes beneath. Strategic portfolios can still be added beside it as comparisons, with their real analysed figures.

What it costs

  • The proposal's portfolio becomes one of two kinds. Today the scenario's base is a strategic portfolio key, and the export, the register, the account form and the file names all read it. Each needs to handle a custom base.
  • No risk figures for the custom portfolio. As with every option here, the files can carry only a weighted-average expected return, an indicative volatility with its upper limit, and the nearest analysed portfolio, each labelled as such.
  • Two rails to build and test, plus a third state of the allocation step: a custom column with comparisons.
  • Effort: large. Roughly twice the custom sleeves build. It is new code beside the old, so strategic proposals keep working exactly as they do.

Is what we built the best approach?

Partly. Custom sleeves weighted as a share of the sleeve are the right tool for implementation, and I would keep them as they are. Changing the allocation through % of the portfolio inside a sleeve is not the best way to customise the allocation, and I would replace it.

Keep

Custom sleeves, % of the sleeve

Products chosen for one proposal at the category's weight. It changes the implementation only, reuses everything downstream, and the controls work.

Change

% of the portfolio

An allocation decision hidden inside an implementation dialog. It works, but it is the wrong place for it, for the five reasons below.

Add

A custom portfolio of its own

Since revision 2: a second kind of proposal, chosen in the Allocation selector and built from nothing within the client's risk profile. Declared tilts serve small changes to a strategic portfolio.

Why % of the portfolio is the wrong place

  1. To change a category's weight you must rebuild its sleeve. A PWA who is happy with the library's Global Equity Core but wants 5 points more equity has to copy it into a custom sleeve just to change its weight. The sleeve choice and the weight are tied together when they should be separate.
  2. The allocation is edited one category at a time, in separate dialogs. Moving 5 points from fixed income to equities means opening two builders and typing product weights in each. The only view of the whole portfolio is a banner saying what it adds to. Option 2 below lets you try it.
  3. Nothing limits how far it goes. A custom sleeve can put a Moderate client at 80% equity. Admins can switch categories on or off, but there is no range, no deviation budget and no approval above a threshold.
  4. The record says what the products were, not what was decided. The register shows each category's strategic and implemented weights, but "+5 points equity, from IG fixed income" is never stated. A reviewer has to work it out.
  5. Dropping the strategic allocation from the files removes the client's reference point. It is the honest answer to having no risk figures for a custom mix, but it is a blunt one. The files could show the strategic portfolio, the proposed one and the difference, with the risk figures clearly labelled as the strategic portfolio's.
The one hard constraint behind every option

Risk and return figures exist only for the 172 strategic portfolios in your universe, computed ahead of time by the analytics library. Neither this tool nor Poros runs that library. For any other allocation the tool can work out a straight-line expected return from the per-asset long-term estimates (19 assets for USD Hedged), and an upper limit on volatility (as if every asset moved together). It cannot work out a true volatility, the stress tests or the tail-loss figures, because the estimates hold no correlations. Every option has to say how it handles this. The options are listed under What the client's files say.

Option 3

Recommended

The PWA changes weights, and a remainder line says how much is left to place. They choose where it goes: in proportion, into one category they pick, or by typing it themselves. Implementation then works as now: library sleeves, or custom sleeves weighted as a share of the sleeve, at the new weights.

Where it goes in the code
  • The scenario gains customAllocation: {category: weightPct}, applied before the overlays. The overlays run on it as on the strategic one, so the tilt and the premium still work.
  • Today's rules.resolveOverlays becomes the second step after applyCustomAllocation, mirrored in the page's JavaScript as the overlays are today.
  • The custom sleeve's % of the portfolio mode goes. Any held sleeve converts to an allocation change plus a % of the sleeve sleeve, which is exactly what it was.
  • Settings become a policy kind beside the custom sleeve settings: ranges by category and risk level, the deviation budget, the approval threshold.
  • Register schema 8: the proposed allocation beside the strategic one. The workbook and deck get a "Proposed allocation" page.
Option 4

Declared tilts

Recommended alongside 3

Instead of typing weights, the PWA states moves: "3 points into Public Equity, from Investment Grade Fixed Income". A tilt is the same thing as an overlay rule, only for one proposal. The engine that runs the tactical tilt and the volatility premium already takes weight from source categories in proportion and puts it somewhere else, so each tilt is a sentence the files can print as written.

ChangesAllocation, as stated moves
WhoAs option 3
FilesStrategic, the tilts, the result
EffortSmall to medium: reuses the overlay engine

For

  • The record is the decision, in words, in order.
  • Always adds to 100%: every tilt takes what it gives.
  • Reuses resolveOverlays, its tests and its funding checks ("an All Equity portfolio holds no IG to fund it").
  • It is how a CIO would describe a tactical view.

Against

  • Awkward for big reshapes: five tilts to rebuild an allocation.
  • Order matters when tilts draw on the same category (as with the overlays today).
Where it goes in the code
  • The scenario gains tilts: [{into, sources, size, basis}], the overlay rule's own shape, minus the toggles and currencies.
  • resolveOverlays(categories, houseRules + proposalTilts): the proposal's tilts run after the overlay rules in force, through the same function.
  • The rail gets a Tilts field beside the overlays. The table shows each tilt's take, the same way the overlays' takes are shown.

Where it starts

  • The Allocation selector lists the strategic allocations for the chosen risk level and type (Full, Core, ex-Alts, …). Below a divider sits Custom: build it yourself. Only people who may build custom portfolios see it: admins, named specialists, and PWAs where an admin has switched custom portfolios on for the type.
  • Choosing it on a proposal with nothing implemented yet goes straight to Implementation, with the blank slate in view. On a proposal that already has sleeves chosen, it first says what happens to them: they are kept, unused, and choosing a strategic allocation again brings them back.
  • All Equity is the one risk level with no allocation choices today, so its rail shows no Allocation selector. For a custom-capable user it shows one, with "All Equity" and "Custom".
  • The risk profile stays chosen and can be changed. Doing so changes the limits and re-runs the checks; the build is never touched.

Filling the blank slate

  • Add products: a search over the products the implementation type may hold. That is the type's own product list (every product its sleeves hold, for PMG ESG the ESG funds), or the whole catalogue where an admin allows it. A product's category comes from its sleeves; a product no sleeve holds is given one suggested from its asset class, which the PWA confirms.
  • Insert a sleeve: a library sleeve's products at a weight of the portfolio the PWA chooses (Global Equity Core at 40% puts its three funds in at 24%, 12% and 4%). After that they are ordinary rows.
  • Paste from Excel: product names, tickers or ids and their weights, checked line by line. A block that does not read cleanly is not taken in (option 7).
  • Copy a strategic portfolio as a draft: a decision for you. It is a quick start, but it brings back an implied starting point. I would leave it out at first: "Insert a sleeve" gives most of the speed without it.

The limits, without a starting point

  • Category ranges from the risk profile: each category's weight in the profile's strategic portfolio, plus or minus. There are two bands. Within the inner band the portfolio delivers without further checks; between the inner and outer bands it needs an admin's approval; beyond the outer band it is refused. Admins set the bands per profile, and can set them per category if wanted.
  • Concentration: no single product above a set share of the portfolio (25% to begin with).
  • Liquidity: products that do not deal daily at most a set share (30%).
  • The usual: adds to exactly 100.00%; every position meets its product's minimum; at most so many products; private markets need a $20m mandate.
  • Approval: outside the inner bands, four-eyes, as in the approval layer. Whether every custom portfolio needs approval regardless is a decision for you.

On the allocation step

  • The allocation table's first column is Custom: each category's weight from the build, and beneath it the products' asset classes (IG Corporate, Municipals, Buyout, …) in place of the strategic portfolios' asset rows.
  • Comparisons work as today. "+" adds strategic portfolios beside the custom one, with their real analysed figures, which is how a PWA shows a client "between Moderate and Moderate-Aggressive, with more private credit".
  • The custom column's figures are the estimated return (a weighted average of the long-term estimates), the volatility limit and the nearest analysed portfolio, each marked as an estimate. Sharpe, the stress tests and tail loss show a dash.
  • Until something is built, the column reads "Not built yet", with a link to Implementation.

Leaving and coming back

  • Back to a strategic allocation: choosing Full (or any other) in the Allocation selector makes the proposal strategic again. The custom build is kept with the scenario, and choosing Custom brings it back as it was. One is in force at a time.
  • A new implementation type keeps the build, and flags any product the new type may not hold. The PWA swaps those products out before it can be delivered. This differs from sleeves, which are cleared on a type change, because a build is too much work to throw away.
  • A new currency or hedging keeps the build. Comparisons are re-resolved in the new basis.
  • A smaller mandate re-runs the minimums at once, and the $20m private markets rule.

What the files and the record say

  • Workbook: a Custom Allocation sheet in place of Portfolios (categories, asset classes, the estimated figures each labelled), then Implementation, and Initial Allocation for a portfolio with private markets. Risk Dashboard and Assumptions are left out, unless the profile's strategic portfolio is carried as a labelled reference.
  • Deck: Cover (reading "Custom portfolio · Moderate risk profile"), Custom Allocation, Implemented Model, Initial Allocation, Composition, and Reference slides if chosen.
  • File names gain "Custom": PMG_Scenario_Custom_USD_Hedged_<date>_<UID>, so a folder of proposals shows which are custom. Today's names do not carry the portfolio at all.
  • Register: the portfolio's kind (custom), the risk profile, every product and weight, who built it, and who approved it if anyone did. It also gets a Custom portfolios view.
  • Account opening form: its risk level is the profile, and its allocation reads "Custom".

What changes underneath

  • The scenario: the base becomes either a strategic key (as now) or {kind: 'custom', profile}, and the build (products and weights) is kept on the scenario. Comparisons stay strategic keys.
  • The server: a custom base is not resolved through the analytics port. The model is built straight from the products, through the same rounding, fees, minimums and funding split, with no overlays. The export, register, account form and file names learn the new kind. A policy kind holds the profile bands and the other limits.
  • The page: the Allocation selector's Custom option; the rail's custom tier on both steps; the builder (what the whole-portfolio prototype shows); the allocation table's custom column.
  • Tests: one that a custom build holding exactly a strategic proposal's products and weights gives the same rows, fees and minimums as that proposal. Then the limits, the rails by kind, the files and the register.
  • Poros: one update, new modules beside the old. No change to the sleeves database. The register gains a column.

For

  • A clear second kind of proposal, chosen where allocations are chosen.
  • No baseline, no "stood at", no confusion about what moved.
  • The rail only shows what applies.
  • Limits that describe the portfolio itself, from the client's risk profile.
  • Sleeves stay useful as building blocks.

Against

  • The largest change: a second kind of portfolio through the whole tool.
  • No risk figures for the custom portfolio itself.
  • Two rails to keep consistent.
  • Small tactical tweaks to a strategic portfolio are not what it is for; tilts (option 4) or custom sleeves still serve those.

Layer

Approval workflow

Applies to any option

A proposal whose total move is above a threshold (the bar's "admin approval above") is held. The PWA submits it with a note, and an admin approves it or sends it back with a reason. Only an approved proposal downloads. Below the threshold nothing changes. The register keeps the whole trail, and the approval can be four-eyes, so the approver cannot be the person who built it.

ChangesWho may deliver
WhoPWA submits, admin decides
FilesUnchanged; the record grows
EffortMedium: states, an inbox, notices

Reuse, for any option

  • Start from a delivered proposal. The register's detail gets "Start a new proposal from this one": the same allocation changes, custom sleeves and tilts, under a new mandate.
  • Save as a model. An admin turns a proposal's changes into a named custom model (option 8), the way "Make it a library sleeve" works today for a custom sleeve.
  • See what is used. The register's views count proposals by model and by tilt, which shows which custom views are becoming house views.
