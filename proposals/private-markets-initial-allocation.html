Private markets: the initial allocation

A portfolio that targets private assets cannot hold them on day one: commitments are called over years. Until they are, the money earmarked for private markets is invested one third in Investment Grade Fixed Income and two thirds in Public Equity. This proposes how the Implementation screen, the workbook and the deck show that initial allocation, and its fees, beside the long-term target — and why the Asset Allocation screen does not.

Scope Implementation screen, workbook, deck, register Not in scope Asset Allocation screen and the strategic sheets and slides Affects 448 of the 688 baked portfolios Would be D136

Decisions taken 28 September 2026. 01 · The rule

What the tool shows today is the long-term target: a book with 9% in private assets shows 9% in private assets, and prices it at private-markets fees. But a commitment to private equity, private credit or real estate is drawn down as managers call capital, and until it is called the money has to be invested somewhere. The rule is fixed:

The private-markets allocation — Private Equity and Other Private Assets together — is initially invested one third in Investment Grade Fixed Income and two thirds in Public Equity. The commitment itself is unchanged: it is still the target amount, and it is drawn from those two positions as capital is called.

So the proposal has two figures for every private-markets book: the target it is built to reach, and the initial allocation it is actually invested in on day one — with the initial one's own fees, which are lower, because the products holding the money are fixed income SMAs and equity ETFs rather than drawdown funds. In the golden case (USD Moderate Full, tilt and volatility premium on, $50m under CASP) the blended fee is 98.6bp at target and 87.9bp initially; under RDR, which prices by fee group, 85.4bp and 70.5bp.

The initial allocation belongs to implementation, not to strategy. The Asset Allocation screen, and the portfolios and risk_dashboard sheets and slides built from it, keep showing the long-term target and its analytics, unchanged — the risk and return figures there are the portfolio's design, not its first month.

03 · The calculation

A third implementation overlay, the same kind of thing as the tactical tilt (D50) and the volatility premium (D53): it reshapes the categories the sleeves are applied to, it lives in rules.py, and the page carries a JavaScript mirror that a test holds to it. Unlike those two it is not a choice — it is how the money is invested — so it has no toggle; it produces a second picture, not a different first one.

  1. The target is built exactly as today. Strategic categories → tactical tilt → volatility premium → sleeves → largest remainder → notionals → fees. Nothing about the target changes.
  2. Take the private line's implemented weight, P. Private Equity and Other Private Assets are already one line against one sleeve (D60, D71); P is that line's weight after both overlays — which never touch it.
  3. Move it: + P/3 to Investment Grade Fixed Income, + 2P/3 to Public Equity; the private line's invested weight is 0. Applied after the tilt and the premium, so the parked money neither funds the tilt nor grows the premium (the premium is 7.5% of IGFI as implemented, and that means the long-term book).
  4. Spread each uplift across the chosen sleeve's products by the sleeve's own shares — the same rule every category weight already follows (product weight = category weight × the product's share of the sleeve). Using the sleeve's shares rather than scaling the positions matters in one case: see edge cases.
  5. Round the initial column on its own by largest remainder across the whole table (spec 8.4), so it closes on exactly 100.00%. Initial notionals derive from the initial printed weights, to the nearest 100, as every notional does.
  6. Price it with the same rates. Management fees resolve per product exactly as for the target — same schedule, level, tier or blend, custom rates — so the difference in the weighted fee comes only from where the money is: product costs of 0.09–0.37% in the parking products against 1.10–1.72% in the drawdown funds, and, under a by-group schedule, the Alternatives fee group's higher rate.
  7. Keep the commitment. The private line's commitment is its target notional; it is what the product minimums are checked against (5,000,000 on every drawdown fund today), so the export gate is unchanged.
initial(category) = target(category)                    for every other category
initial(IGFI)     = target(IGFI)   + P × 1/3
initial(Equity)   = target(Equity) + P × 2/3
initial(Private)  = 0                                    commitment = target notional
product weight    = initial(category) × product's share of the sleeve
printed           = largest remainder over the whole column, 2dp, closes on 100.00
notional          = round(mandate × printed / 100, to 100)
weighted fee (bp) = (product cost % + management fee %) × printed %

The split and the categories are rules, not code: rules.PRIVATE_FUNDING — the sleeve group funded from, and [(IGFI, 1/3), (Public Equity, 2/3)] — served in the schema's rules block so the page reads it rather than restating it, as it already does for the tilt and the premium.

04 · On the Implementation screen

Only where the book being implemented holds private assets. For every other book — ex-Alts, Core, all-equity, 240 of the 688 baked portfolios — nothing on the screen changes and no control appears.

A view switch above the table

Long-term target | Initial allocation, beside the table's head. It changes what the table, its totals, the fee line and the doughnuts show; it changes nothing about the proposal, so it is a view, not scenario state — not saved, not sent, not in the register. Opens on the long-term target, the screen as PWAs know it.

In the initial view, every changed figure says what it was

A parking product shows its initial weight and notional with target 10.82% beneath in the small type the table already uses for min $5,000,000; the private band and its products show 0.00% invested with committed $4,500,000 beneath. Nothing has to be compared across two views.

A “Private markets funding” note in the rail

Below the overlays, where the tilt and premium explain themselves: the commitment in % and $, and where it is held until called — ⅓ and ⅔, in % and $, with a small bar. Information, not a control: there is no choice to make.

Both fees, always

The fee line gives the blended weighted fee at target and initially, the difference in bp and in dollars a year on the mandate — in either view, because the question it answers (“what will I pay?”) has two honest answers.

Doughnuts follow the view

The five composition doughnuts are built from whichever picture the table shows. In the initial view the Drawdown slice leaves Liquidity and Internal/Passive grow — which is the point.

Gates and pickers unchanged

Completion still counts sleeves; the export still refuses a target position below its minimum. The initial allocation only ever adds to IGFI and equity positions, so it cannot create a breach — and a commitment below a fund's minimum is still a breach even though nothing is invested in it yet.

06 · In the deck

A new slide, Initial Implementation, directly after Implemented Model, only when the book holds private assets. It is the same Doc as the sheet, planned by planDeck like the implementation table — fitted to its slide, paginated if it must be, every rule drawn from both sides (D127), the indents the implementation table uses (D135). The subheading states the commitment and the split; the footnotes say how the weights were derived and that fees are charged on what is invested. 07 · State, register and wire

WhereChange
Scenario stateNone. The initial allocation is derived from the target by a fixed rule; there is nothing to store and nothing to PUT. The screen's view switch is not state either.
Schemarules.privateFunding: the group funded from and the two destinations with their shares. The page reads it with App.opt, as it reads the tilt's and the premium's.
The modelbuildImplementationRows returns model['initial'] beside today's groups and total — groups, total (weight, notional, weighted fee) and commitment (weight, notional, the two held amounts) — or None for a book without private assets. Built once, in the same call, from the same rates (D69).
ExportNo new route and no new argument: the workbook and deck writers add their sheet and slide when model['initial'] is present. One export, one UID, both files (D123).
RegisterThe implemented picture it already stores as JSON gains an initial key: per product the initial weight and notional, plus the commitment. No schema change; proposals delivered before carry none, which is the truth about them.
CyrusRe-copy the package and the page folder (PORTING.md §0A). No dependency, setting, route or register migration.

08 · Edge cases, from the data

CaseWhat happens
No private assetsex-Alts, Core, all-equity: 240 of 688 baked portfolios. No switch, no rail note, no sheet, no slide. Byte-for-byte today's files.
Tilt empties IGFI32 of the 448 private-holding books hold exactly 8% IGFI, which the tactical tilt takes entirely. Their target has no IGFI row, but the initial allocation must still put P/3 there. Because the uplift is spread by the sleeve's shares, not by scaling existing positions, it works: EUR Higher Risk Full with the tilt shows IGFI 0.00% → 3.00%. It does mean the IGFI sleeve must be chosen even when the tilt leaves IGFI at zero — completion counts it for these books. (Try it in the demo.)
Volatility premiumThe parked third goes to Investment Grade Fixed Income only, not Hybrid Fixed Income, and the premium's 7.5% is of the long-term IGFI — the overlay runs after it.
Size of the effectPrivate markets are 5.0–10.1% of the books that hold them (Full and ex-HFs); the largest, USD Moderate-Aggressive ex-HFs ex-RAs, moves 3.37% to IGFI and 6.74% to equity.
Sleeve not yet chosenAs today: rows for a category without a sleeve have no products, and the initial view is as incomplete as the target — the same count, the same gate.
Fees excluded / custom / marginalThe initial table follows the target's pricing exactly: unpriced when fees are excluded (columns gone), the PWA's rates under the custom level (D96), the mandate's blend on every row under a marginal schedule (D83). Under a marginal schedule only product costs move the blend.
MinimumsCommitments are checked at target, as now. Parking only increases IGFI and equity positions, so no new breach is possible.
RoundingEach column is rounded on its own and each closes on 100.00%. A product outside the moved categories can differ by 0.01% between the two columns where the remainders fall differently — the same property the page and workbook already have, and the reason each column is rounded rather than derived from the other.

09 · How it would be built

  1. Rules and model. rules.PRIVATE_FUNDING and rules.initialCategories(); buildImplementationRows builds model['initial'] after the target. Tests: the split, the order after both overlays, the tilt-empties-IGFI case, closure on 100.00, pricing under every schedule kind, no change to any book without private assets.
  2. The page. initialCategories() and the initial rows in implementation.js (mirror, held to Python by the existing mirror-test pattern on the same fixtures); the view switch, the small-type annotations, the rail note, the fee line, the doughnuts following the view; schema rules.privateFunding.
  3. Workbook and deck. sheetDoc.buildInitialImplementationDoc; the Excel writer adds the sheet after Implementation; planDeck adds the slide after Implemented Model. Tests: sheet and slide present only with private assets, columns and totals, the two preamble lines, the deck walked cell for cell against its plan as every table is.
  4. Register. implementedPicture gains initial; the record pane shows it. Tests: round-trip, and a pre-D136 row reading as “not recorded”.
  5. Docs. D136 in DEVIATIONS.md; PORTING.md §0A (package and page re-copy, nothing else) and the acceptance checklist (the sixth sheet, the extra slide).

10 · For you to decide

1 · Where the parked money goes inside IGFI and Public Equity

Pro rata across the chosen sleeves' products, as every category weight is spread — or into one designated product per category (a short-duration fund, a broad index ETF), which would read more like a cash-management position.

Recommended: pro rata. It needs no new product choice and keeps the sleeve the PWA chose as the only thing deciding what is held.

2 · Workbook and deck: a separate sheet and slide, or extra columns

A separate Initial Implementation sheet and slide (one Doc, both renderers) — or Initial Notional / Initial (%) / Initial fee columns added to the existing Implementation sheet and slide.

Recommended: separate. The implementation slide is already 12 columns at 8.5–9.25pt; three more would push it below comfortable reading. One Doc keeps the sheet and the slide identical.

3 · The screen's default view

Open on the long-term target (today's screen) or on the initial allocation (what is bought first).

Recommended: long-term target, with the initial one click away and both fees always shown.

4 · Doughnuts in the downloads

Keep the downloads' doughnuts as the target's only, or add an initial composition to the new sheet and slide.

Recommended: target only. The composition of the long-term book is what the doughnuts are for; the initial table already shows what moved.

5 · Always on, or a PWA toggle

Show the initial allocation for every book that holds private assets, or let a PWA switch it off for a proposal.

Recommended: always on. It is how the money will be invested and what it will cost at first; a proposal without it would overstate the day-one fee.

Set aside

  • The path, not just the start. How the parked money is drawn down over time — a pacing model of capital calls, distributions and recommitments — would turn one initial picture into a schedule. The rule given covers the starting point only, and a pacing model needs assumptions the tool does not hold.
  • Over-commitment. Committing more than the target to reach it sooner is a common practice this proposal does not model.
  • The web page's other surfaces. The register's record pane shows the stored initial picture; the Asset Allocation screen and the strategic analytics do not change, by design.
