The initial allocation, as columns

Your five decisions applied to the proposal: the initial allocation is three extra columns — weight, notional and weighted fee — that a button above the implementation table slides in and out, and that the workbook and the deck always carry for a private-markets book. Three placements to choose between, each working on real portfolios below.

1 · DECIDEDSpread across the chosen sleeves⅓ to IGFI, ⅔ to Public Equity, by each sleeve's product shares.
2 · DECIDEDExtra columns, not a new sheet or slideIn the table on screen, in the workbook's Implementation sheet and on the deck's Implemented Model slide.
3 · DECIDEDThe screen opens on the long-term targetThe initial columns are hidden until the button shows them.
4 · DECIDEDTarget compositions onlyThe doughnuts are the long-term book's, on screen and in the files.
5 · DECIDEDAlways on for private-asset booksThe button appears for every one; the downloads always carry the columns.
How I have read 5 against 3. The button is a view on the screen: it opens hidden, it is not saved and it does not change the proposal. The workbook and the deck carry the initial columns for every private-markets book whether or not the button was pressed — a proposal should not change with a view. If you would rather the downloads follow the button, that is a one-line change and is noted in the decisions at the end.

Try it

Pick a placement, then press Show initial allocation above the table. The columns slide in over 360ms — the same motion the fee columns already use (D52) — and slide out again; with reduced motion set, they simply appear. Pick USD Moderate ex-Alts to see the button disappear for a book with no private assets. Every figure is the tool's own chain on the delivered bake, held to the Python prototype to the cent.

How the button behaves

AspectBehaviour
When it appearsOnly when the book being implemented holds private assets — Private Equity or Other Private Assets with weight after the overlays. For every other book (240 of 688 baked) there is no button, no hidden column and no change to the table.
WhereIn a toolbar directly above the table, left-aligned, with a one-line note beside it: the commitment in % and $, and “held ⅓ in IGFI, ⅔ in Public Equity until capital is called”. The note is there whether the columns are shown or not, so a PWA knows why the button exists.
Default and memoryHidden on arrival (decision 3). It stays as the PWA left it while they work on the scenario, and resets to hidden on the next visit — a view, never saved to the scenario or the register.
The motionThe columns are always in the table, collapsed to zero width; the button widens them over 360ms, the fee reveal's own duration (D52), and a group row fades in above the header labelling Long-term target and Initial allocation. Reversing it plays the same motion back. Respects reduced motion.
Telling the two apartThree cues, any one of which would do: the teal header and tint of the initial columns against the navy long-term ones; the group row naming each; and a thin teal rule at the block's left edge. Figures the rule moved are bold teal; the private rows read 0.00% and $0 with called over time beneath.
The fee lineAbove the table: the long-term blended fee always, and when the columns are shown, the initial one with the difference in bp and dollars a year sliding in beside it.
AccessibilityA real <button> with aria-pressed and aria-controls; its label changes between Show and Hide initial allocation. Collapsed cells are aria-hidden, so a screen reader never meets an invisible column.
What it does not touchCompletion, the export gate (still on the long-term commitments' minimums), the sleeve pickers, the doughnuts (long-term only), and step 1.

Left for you

Which placement — A, B or C

A keeps day one in one place: its weight, its money and its cost read together, and the block appears and disappears as a unit. B is best for comparing one column with its twin, but splits the initial figures into three places. C keeps each fee next to each fee.

Recommended: A.

The slide: all 15 columns at 8.0pt, or 13 at 8.5pt

All columns keep the slide identical to the sheet at the deck's smallest size; dropping Share Class and Source (slide only) buys half a point and some air.

Recommended: 13 at 8.5pt — the two columns are the least read on a slide, and both remain in the workbook.

Downloads: always, or following the button

As read above, the files always carry the columns for a private-markets book. The alternative is that they appear only if the PWA had the columns shown when downloading.

Recommended: always — a proposal should not depend on a view.

Labels

Headers Initial (%), Initial notional, Initial fee on screen (Initial Wtd fee (bp) in the files), under the group label Initial allocation · until capital is called (shortened to Initial allocation or Initial where it spans fewer columns); the long-term group reads Long-term target.
