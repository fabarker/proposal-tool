The Repository’s Fees tab, taken ten ways. The card underneath does not change: 2 schedules, 5 fee groups, 5 account-size tiers, 6 levels — 180 rates, edited all at once, saved with a note as a new version, and refused if someone else saved first.

FirstWhat every design keeps

These are ten layouts of one card and one set of rules. What varies is how much of the card you see at once, where you type, and how groups and tiers are changed.

The same card

CASP prices every product with one blended rate; RDR prices each product by its fee group. Each has six levels — Management and PMG at Floor, Target and Ceiling — in each of five tiers.

The same save

Edits gather in a draft. Review lists every change; a note is required; the save is a new version in the history, and a stale draft is refused rather than overwriting.

The same checks

Floor ≤ Target ≤ Ceiling on every band, every rate filled, tiers that join up, and no removing a group that still prices products — whichever layout the message lands in.

The same door

Admins only, on the Repository’s Fees tab, with Import a file, Download the card and the history alongside. Step 2’s fee viewer stays read only.

FinallyWhat I would build

Design 2, with design 9’s paste as a later addition if real rates arrive as a spreadsheet. It is the only one that shows a whole family — every group, every tier — on one screen without shrinking the numbers, and it puts the tier edges and group names where they act, in the table’s own headers, so the two panels below the grid go away. The target leads because it is the rate a proposal is priced at; floor and ceiling sit beneath it, one click from editing. If seeing all 180 at once matters more than the reading order, design 1 is the cheapest to build and nothing on it is ever hidden.
