Custom fees

A third fee level beside Management and PMG. Choosing it opens an editable fee card: the delivered rates for reference, and a Custom column the PWA fills, one rate per schedule and fee group. Every product in a group pays that group's rate.

The card, in detail

It is the rate-card viewer (D55) in its by tier pivot, pinned to this mandate, with one column added. The reader who knows the viewer knows this card.

Rows

One per schedule and fee group, the viewer's own list: CASP as one row, then RDR with a row for each of its five fee groups. Each row says how many of this model's products it prices, so a group with none is visibly optional.

The rows of the schedule chosen in the rail are live. The other schedule's rows stay on the card for reference but their Custom cell is inert, with the reason on hover: choose RDR to price by fee group.

Reference columns

Management Floor / Target / Ceiling, then PMG Floor / Target / Ceiling: six columns of delivered rates. The RDR rows show the tier this mandate's top account size falls in, named in the card's head. The CASP row shows the blend for this mandate at each level, since CASP is marginal (D83) and a single tier would be the wrong number to copy.

Every reference cell on a live row is a button: click it and it becomes that row's custom rate.

The Custom column

Far right, ruled off, one input per live row. Percent to two decimals, the unit of every other fee; 45bp is accepted and shown as 0.45%. Under each input, the product count it applies to.

Fill from, in the tool strip, copies one delivered level into every live row at once; the PWA then edits the rows that differ. Clear empties the column.

Foot and actions

What the column prices: rows set, effective management fee, all-in weighted fee in basis points, and dollars a year, live as cells are entered. Apply writes the column to the scenario; Cancel and Escape discard the draft. A partial column can be applied: the products of a group without a rate are unpriced, and the rail and the table say which.

Rules

Grain

A custom rate belongs to a schedule and fee group, never to a product. Every product in the group pays it; a product cannot differ from its group. Under CASP the group is the whole book.

Range

0.00% to 3.00% inclusive, a rule served in the schema (rules.customFeeMax), not a number in the page. Outside it is refused with the reason and the previous value kept. Zero is a real rate and prints as 0.00%.

A blank is unpriced

A live row without a rate leaves its products with no management fee: dash in the cell, no weighted fee, the total unpriced, the download refused with Set a custom rate for every fee group in the model. It never reads as zero. Rows with no products in the model may stay blank.

Each schedule keeps its own

The custom rates for CASP and for RDR are stored side by side. Switching the schedule under Custom shows the other set; switching the level back to PMG or Management keeps both, as a stored schedule survives an untick today (D52), so returning to Custom restores them.

Sleeves can change under it

Rates are keyed by group, so a product that arrives in a priced group is priced at once, and one that leaves changes nothing. A product that arrives in a group with no rate is unpriced and the rail says which group. No rate is ever carried from one group to another.

The tier is this mandate's

The card is pinned to the tier the top account size falls in (RDR) and to the mandate's blend (CASP). There is no tier selector on the editable card; the viewer keeps its own for browsing the delivery.

The server prices

fees.productFee gains a custom branch that reads the group's rate; resolveFee on the page mirrors it; the mirror test walks every schedule and group. The page never computes a fee it was not served.

The rounding rule holds

Weighted fee is the printed all-in times the printed weight, and the total is the sum (§8.4). Custom changes an input to that arithmetic, not the arithmetic.

The record says so

The workbook's header rows read Fee schedule: RDR · Fee level: Custom · entered by A. Castellanos, 20 Sep 2026 · against card 0.0-placeholder, then one line per group and rate. The register's implemented picture carries the same.

Who may

Anyone who may edit the scenario. A cap below the range, or an approval before download, would be a capability in the schema, off by default, and a proposal of its own.

On the wire

One new value for an existing field, and one new field. Everything else the scenario carries is unchanged.

PUT /api/scenario/{id}
{
  "feeSchedule": "RDR",              // unchanged: CASP or RDR
  "feeLevel":    "Custom",           // new value beside the six delivered levels
  "customFees": {                    // new; each schedule keeps its own
    "CASP": 0.45,                    // one rate, the whole book
    "RDR":  { "Passive": 0.10, "Core Active": 0.38, "Specialist Active": 0.56,
              "Alternatives": 0.76, "Asset Allocation": 0.26 }
  }
}

validateFeeLevel accepts Custom; a rate outside rules.customFeeMax or a group the card does not name is refused with the field. GET /scenario/schema already serves the reference rates the card needs (fees.rates, blended under CASP), so the card opens with no fetch, as the build-up card does (D84).

How it would be built

  1. Datafees.json gains "customLevel": "Custom" and "customFeeMax": 3.0; the store gains customFees with the shape above, accepted by PUT /scenario/{id} and validated in rules.py beside validateFeeLevel.
  2. Pricingfees.productFee reads the group's custom rate when the level is Custom, or None when the group has none. workbook.implementationModel passes customFees through; the header rows gain the Custom lines.
  3. RailfeeFields draws a third source button, hides the point row under Custom, and draws the summary block and Edit custom fees. The change handlers write feeLevel as they do for the six levels.
  4. CardA new panel beside renderFeePanel and renderPricePanel, built from the viewer's by-tier table with the Custom column, the tool strip, the foot and Apply / Cancel. Same focus, Escape and inert handling as the other two (§13). Opens itself on the first choice of Custom; the rail button reopens it.
  5. TableNo change to the columns. resolveFee gains the branch; the guide marks nothing here, since the entry happens in the card.
  6. RecordSpec §8.3 (the model), §9.3 (the column under Custom), §10.3 (the refusal), §11 (the wire); a DEVIATIONS entry; the register's implemented picture gains the rates.

For you to decide

  1. Both schedules on the card, or only the chosen one? The mock shows both, the unchosen one inert, because that is the viewer's shape and it lets a PWA see what RDR would have charged while pricing under CASP. Showing only the chosen schedule's rows makes a shorter card under CASP: one row.
  2. Open the card on the first choice of Custom? The mock does. The alternative is to show the summary block with Edit custom fees and let the PWA open it, which is one more click and no surprise.
  3. Groups with no products. The mock lets them stay blank. Requiring every row would make the card a complete custom schedule, reusable if the sleeves change; the cost is entering rates for products that are not there.
  4. The ceiling. 3.00% is a guess.
  5. Who entered it, on the client-facing sheet. The name and date could go only to the register.
