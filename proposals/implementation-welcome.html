Welcoming the implementation step

The first time in a session that a PWA opens Implementation, dim the workspace and hold them at a card explaining that sleeves are chosen in the rail. Five ways to do it, each with a working mock-up, plus the rules a blocking modal has to follow.

Scope step 2, on arrivalBranch sleeve-editionsStatus for review, nothing built

What happens today

Clicking Implementation swaps the document for the implementation table and replaces the rail's base-portfolio tier with the Sleeves tier. Nothing announces that change. Since D95 the first empty sleeve picker carries the guide's mark and focus moves down the rail as each is answered, but a PWA looking at the document may not notice a mark in the panel beside it.

The request: a modal on the first visit per session, dismissed only by OK.

The mock-ups

Each mock is the workspace at the moment of the click. Press Click “Implementation” to replay the arrival; the card behaves as it would in the tool — Escape and clicks outside do nothing, only OK closes it, and focus is held inside until it does. All five share the trigger and the trap; they differ in what the card says and where it sits.

A

The plain card

Smallest buildCentredStatic copy

A centred card over a dimmed workspace: a heading, two sentences, and OK. It says where the sleeve pickers are and what they do. Nothing about it depends on the portfolio, so it is one block of copy and a flag.

How it works

  • Shown once per browser session (sessionStorage), on a click in the step nav, never on a reload that lands on step 2.
  • Scrim plus role="dialog" aria-modal="true"; the rail and document go inert, as they do for the mandate dialog.
  • OK is the only way out. Escape and outside clicks are ignored, and focus returns to the rail's first picker.

Watch for

  • It says "categories" in the abstract while the categories are on screen behind it. B fixes that.
  • A modal that cannot be escaped is unusual: it needs a reason a reader can see, which is why the copy names what to do next rather than welcoming them.
B

The card that lists this portfolio's categories

Small buildCentredReads the model

The same card, but the body is the four categories this base portfolio actually holds, with their weights, and a line saying how many need a sleeve. The PWA reads the work before doing it, and the card is visibly about their proposal rather than about the tool.

How it works

  • The list is sleeveCategories(baseCategories()) minus the auto-attached ones — the same list the rail draws.
  • A category already answered (a rehydrated scenario, or a second visit in a new session) shows a tick, and the count says "2 of 4 still need one".

Watch for

  • Seven categories at the widest make a long card. It scrolls, or the list caps and says "and 3 more".
  • If every category already has a sleeve, the card has nothing to ask for — it should not appear at all.
C

The three-step card

Small buildCentredMore reading

A numbered summary of the whole step: attach a sleeve to each category, price the model if the proposal shows fees, download the workbook. It answers "what am I doing here" rather than only "what do I do now".

How it works

  • Static copy, three items, the first one emphasised as the immediate task.
  • Pairs with a Show this again control in the rail if a PWA wants it back.

Watch for

  • Three things to read before the first click. For a PWA on their tenth proposal that is two too many, and they see it every session.
  • Steps 2 and 3 are not blocked on anything; describing them here risks reading as a checklist that must be done in order.
D

Spotlight the rail, card beside it

Medium buildStrongest pointerNarrow screens

Only the document dims. The rail stays lit, with a ring around it, and the card sits against the rail's edge with a pointer into it. The thing being talked about is the only thing the PWA can see clearly, so the card barely has to say where to look.

How it works

  • The scrim covers the document only; the rail is raised above it and ringed in --rail-focus, the colour the guide already uses.
  • The card is positioned against the rail with a pointer, and the OK button is still the only way out.
  • The rail is lit but still inert — nothing behind the card is clickable until OK.

Watch for

  • Below 1040px the rail stacks above the document, so the card has no side to sit on: it falls back to A's centred position and the spotlight covers the panel above instead.
  • Lighting the rail while keeping it inert can read as "click here" — the card has to say OK first, which it does.
E

Card, then hand over to the guide

Small buildJoins up with D95Recommended

B's card, and OK is a handover: the card closes, the rail's first empty picker takes focus and its mark, and the document says which category is being answered. The modal stops being a notice and becomes the first move of the flow the tool already has.

How it works

  • OK reads Choose the first sleeve, and closing focuses the picker D95 has marked — the same helper, so the two can never disagree about which is first.
  • If every category already has a sleeve, the card does not appear; there is nothing to hand over to.

Watch for

  • Focus landing in a native select means the next keypress may open it — which is the intent here, but it is a change in where the keyboard is pointing and should be announced.

E with D’s spotlight

The same card and the same handover, with only the document dimmed: the rail stays lit and ringed, and the card sits against its edge with a pointer into it. This is the combination recommended below, without F’s strip.

OK still hands focus to the first picker; the rail is lit but inert until then.

Two non-blocking alternatives

Both were mocked for comparison. Neither meets the "forced to click OK" requirement, and both are here because a blocking modal on every session is the strongest instrument available and it is worth seeing what the weaker ones look like.

F · A strip above the table. Always present until every category has a sleeve, so it teaches by staying rather than by blocking. It cannot be missed on arrival but it can be ignored forever.

G · A toast at the foot. Dismisses itself after eight seconds. Cheapest and least intrusive; also the easiest to miss, which is the thing this proposal is trying to fix.

Rules, whichever is chosen

Once per session

sessionStorage, one key, with an in-page fallback where storage is refused. A new tab greets again; a reload in the same tab does not.

Only on a click

The step nav's Implementation button. A reload that lands on step 2, or a rehydrated scenario that was left there, shows nothing — the PWA did not just arrive.

Nothing to say, nothing shown

If every category already has a sleeve, there is no instruction to give and the card is skipped, whatever the flag says.

OK is the only exit

Escape and outside clicks are ignored, and the card has no close button. This is a deliberate departure from every other dialog in the tool (§13), which is why it is once per session and never on a return visit.

Focus is trapped and returned

Focus moves to OK on open and is held inside the card. On close it goes to the rail's first empty picker (E), or back to the step-nav button that opened it.

It is announced

role="dialog" aria-modal="true", labelled by its heading, with the rail and document inert — the same handling the mandate dialog and the fee cards use.

Motion

A short fade on the scrim and a small scale on the card, as the other dialogs have. It plays on every platform (D90).

A way back to it

Optional: a quiet What is this step? link under the sleeves tier heading reopens the card any time, which makes dismissing it cheap.

Recommended: E, with D's spotlight if you want it louder

E is B's card — the categories this portfolio holds, and how many need a sleeve — with OK reading Choose the first sleeve and handing focus to the picker the guide has already marked. It blocks as asked, it is about the proposal on screen rather than about the tool, and it ends by putting the PWA's cursor on the first thing to do. D's spotlight is a single extra rule on the scrim and a raised rail; it can be added to E without changing the copy, and it is the right call if the rail is being missed even after the card.

I would also take F as a companion: the card teaches once per session, the strip stays until the work is done, and the two never contradict each other because both read the same count.

E with D's spotlight, and F's strip left behind after OK. Press Click “Implementation” to replay.

How it would be built

  1. The flagsessionStorage key pmg.proposalTool.implGreeted, with an in-page fallback, set when the card is dismissed. Read in the step-nav click handler in js/implementation.js, beside where D94's flash used to be armed.
  2. The panelA fourth host beside #mandateDialog, #feeDialog and #customDialog, rendered by renderGreetPanel(). It reuses .scrim and .dialog, adds no close button, and ignores Escape and scrim clicks — the one dialog in the tool that does.
  3. The bodyThe categories come from the same helper the rail uses, so the card and the rail cannot disagree. Skipped entirely when none needs a sleeve.
  4. The handoverOK closes, then focuses the picker sleeveNext() marks (D95), through the same focusOnClose flush the fee dialogs use.
  5. Record itSpec §7.1 (the arrival sequence) and §13.2 (the one dialog Escape does not close), plus a DEVIATIONS entry.

For you to decide

  1. Spotlight or plain dim? E alone, or E with D's lit rail.
  2. Once per session, or once per scenario? Per session as asked. Per scenario (like D95's guide) would greet a PWA once for each proposal they build, which is more often.
  3. A way back to it? A What is this step? link in the sleeves tier, or nothing.
  4. Should F's strip ship too? It is the part that keeps working after the card is gone.
  5. The copy. The mock-ups use my wording; it is the cheapest thing to change and the most worth getting right.
