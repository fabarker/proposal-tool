Open an Account, UID first

The card opens asking for one thing: the Proposal UID. Once it resolves, the card grows into the request itself, with everything the proposal already knows filled in and the rest waiting for you. Five ways the second half could look, one set of states they share, and a recommendation.

Where we areToday the card shows everything at once

Press Open an Account and the card opens at full length before a single character is typed: the UID box, then a greyed From the proposal block of eight read-only boxes and a sleeve list, then a greyed Account details block of nine fields, then the footer. Eighteen boxes and a sleeve list, and only the first box does anything.

Why that hurts. The only thing you can do is type a UID, but the card makes you read past a wall of empty grey to understand that. Disabled fields look broken rather than pending, and on a laptop screen the card overflows and scrolls inside itself — so the UID field, the one live thing, can be the first thing that scrolls away.

What stays the same in every option below. The lookup (GET /scenario/proposals/{uid}), the nine fields and their rules, the one-request-per-proposal refusal, and the receipt. This is a change to the card's shape, not to what it submits.

One gap worth knowing about. The proposal does not record a client name — the mandate card never asks for one. So the thing a reader would use first to recognise the right proposal is not on it. Confirming the match has to rely on the PWA, the mandate size, the basis and the export date. That is why every option below keeps those four in plain sight.

Stage 1 · the same in every optionThe card opens small

One field, one line of help, and Cancel. The card is the width of the mandate card, 460px, and short enough that nothing scrolls. Each state below happens in this small card; it only grows when the UID resolves to a proposal that can still take a request.

What it gains

  • One continuous movement: the thing you typed stays where you typed it, and the rest appears beneath it.
  • The four facts you check the match against sit at the top, readable at a glance, before a single field asks anything of you.
  • The digest cuts eight boxes to eight lines of text. The card is shorter than today's.
  • The From mandate tag makes the one prefilled field visibly different from the ones you typed.

What it costs

  • The card changes width as well as height, 460 to 560. A small jump — see the decision on width below.
  • Still a long card. On a 768px-tall laptop the footer will scroll.
  • Sleeves are one click away rather than visible.

Worth decidingFour choices the mock-ups make for you

What makes it expand: the UID alone, or a button?

Today the lookup fires 150ms after the box holds a well-formed UID. Keeping that means pasting a UID is all it takes — the card grows under your cursor. A Find proposal button would make the expansion something you asked for, at the cost of a click that does nothing but confirm a paste.

I'd keep it automatic, plus Enter. UIDs are pasted, not typed, so the moment the box is full is the moment you meant.

What happens to typed details if the UID is changed?

Press Change after filling half the form. Either the account fields are cleared, since they were meant for another proposal, or they are kept and the card collapses to the UID with them held in memory.

I'd keep them and say so — Your account details are kept under the UID box. The usual reason to change a UID is a typo or the wrong re-export, and the client is the same client.

One width throughout, or grow wider?

The small card at 460px matches the mandate card and looks right with one field. The full form needs 560px for its two columns. Opening at 560 avoids the sideways jump but leaves a wide card with one short field in it.

I'd grow both ways, in one 260ms movement, height and width together. One transition reads as one event; it is the width change on its own, after the height, that looks like a glitch.

Should the mandate card start asking for a client name?

Outside this card's scope, but it is the root of why matching a UID to a proposal is hard: the register has no client on it. If the mandate carried one, every option here could lead with Ashworth Family Trust instead of a hex string, and the account's name field could prefill.

Worth a separate look. It changes the mandate card, the register and the workbook, so it is its own piece of work.

If you want oneRecommendation

Option 01, grow in place, with 04's safeguard folded into the footer rather than given its own step.

  1. It is the literal shape of what you asked for. The card opens on the UID alone, and the same card grows into the request once it resolves — no mode change, no new title, no wizard.
  2. The digest is the real improvement. The four facts you match the proposal against are readable at the top, as text rather than eight grey boxes, and the card ends up shorter than today's.
  3. The one-request rule stops being a surprise. State 1e answers this proposal already has a request in the small card, before anything expands — today it expands the whole form and then disables it.
  4. It keeps 04's protection without 04's extra click. The Ready line names the PWA and mandate beside the UID, so the last thing read before Submit is the thing you would check.

If the wrong-proposal mistake has actually happened, or you expect PWAs to hold several re-exports of the same scenario, 04 is the honest upgrade: the confirm step costs a click and buys certainty. If you mainly want this done quickly, 05 delivers the requirement in an afternoon and can be improved into 01 later.
