Sixteen columns, one visual weight

Nine are text attributes, and most barely vary. Share is Dis on 57 of 73 rows, Source is Internal on 60, Style is Active on 60, Liquidity is Daily on 54, Ccy is USD on 43. Most of the ink repeats the usual answer.

The figures start off-screen

Measured in a 1,400px window: the dialog is 1,296px, the table is given 1,086px and needs 1,196px. Net, Min and Used sit to the right of the fold. When a product's panel opens, it lands on top of every figure column.

The quiet columns are too quiet

Five columns print in #8494A4 on white. That is 3.1:1; text at 12.5px needs 4.5:1. The next grey in the palette, #5B6B7C, is 5.5:1. So the secondary text is both hard to read and still in the way.

Two boxes a row that say “normal”

Every row wears a vehicle tag and a liquidity pill: 73 × 2 boxes, and 54 of the pills say Daily. Net is green on almost every row. Tickers are bold, though 38 of 73 products have none.

Headers that need decoding

VEH, SRC, CCY, LIQ, MGMT @T3: abbreviations, set in 10.5px letter-spaced condensed capitals, in the lightest grey. Five figure columns stand side by side and none states a unit.

No landmarks

73 rows at one height under identical hairlines. A row is about 1,100px long from its name to its last figure, and only a faint hover (#F7F9FB on white) helps the eye hold it.

How to read the ten. Options 1 to 6 keep all sixteen columns and change how they are drawn. Options 7 to 10 change what is on screen. Every example moves one lever and leaves the rest exactly as it is today, including things another option would fix, so that what you are judging is that lever alone.

Table examples are drawn 1,086px wide, the width the table has today, so the sideways scroll is honest. Where an example says 37 rows it is every other product of the all-in order, which keeps the real proportions of ETFs, SMAs, drawdown funds and unused products.

Colour by exception

Colour and contrastAll 16 columns staySmall change

The problem. The view's stylesheet states its own rule: colour is only ever a constraint or a selection. In practice every row carries a grey vehicle tag, a liquidity pill and a green net figure. In the 37 rows below that is 111 boxed or coloured marks, nearly all of them saying nothing unusual here. The marks that matter (Quarterly, Drawdown, a product no sleeve holds) are the same size and shape as the ones that do not.

The change. Finish the rule. The house defaults (Daily, Dis, Active, Internal, USD) print as plain text, in the grey that passes contrast. A value that departs from its default prints in full ink. Only constraints keep a pill or a colour: liquidity that is not daily, a negative net, zero use. Positive net loses its green, the vehicle loses its box. 111 marks become 24, and the 29 departures from a default read darker than their neighbours.

What it gains

  • The eye lands on the 24 cells that carry a constraint, not on the 111 that say nothing.
  • The quiet columns become readable: 5.5:1 where they are 3.1:1.
  • No layout change, no column moves. Three cases in catCellHtml and a handful of rules.

What it costs

  • “Plain means normal” has to be learned. A newcomer loses some texture that the boxes gave for free.
  • A default is a judgement. Internal and Daily are easy; whether Passive is an exception someone has to own, per column.
  • Vehicle has no default among its five values, so it simply becomes plain text.

A type hierarchy instead of boxes

Typography and hierarchyAll 16 columns staySmall change

The problem. Everything is 12.5px. Hierarchy is carried by grey against black and by boxes, so the name, the ticker and the all-in figure all speak at one volume, the ticker in bold though 38 of 73 rows have none. The headers, already abbreviations, are set in the least legible way the page has: 10.5px condensed capitals, letter-spaced, in the lightest grey. Figures are condensed too, the width at which 3, 8 and 0 are hardest to tell apart.

The change. Three levels and nothing else. One: the product name, 13px semibold. Two: the figure the list is sorted on, bold. Three: everything else, regular. Headers move to sentence case at 11.5px semibold and are spelled out where the cells beneath already give them room (Vehicle, Source, Liquidity). The ticker drops to regular weight and a missing ticker fades to a pale dash. Figures move to the regular-width face with tabular numerals. Colours and pills are untouched here; that is option 1's lever.

Try it. Click rows, or Tab into the list and use ↑ ↓. Open the filter chips.

What it gains

  • The record never covers the list, and never has to be asked for.
  • The list can afford to be calm, because the detail is one glance to the right.
  • Thirty-five checkboxes leave the first impression of the page.

What it costs

  • The largest build of the ten: catalogueViewHtml, the facet rail, and the panel's place in the layout.
  • Facet counts are one click away instead of always on show, and filters in force must appear as chips or they will be forgotten.
  • The list compares six fields at a glance, not sixteen. Across-the-board comparison leans on Pin and Compare, which exist.
  • Under about 1,100px the inspector has to become today's overlay again.
