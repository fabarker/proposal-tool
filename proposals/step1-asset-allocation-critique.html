Proposal Tool · design critique

Step 1 — Strategic Asset Allocation

What the screen is for, where it slows its user down, and 24 specific changes. Studied from the generator source (core.js, picker.js, build_styles.py) rather than screenshots, so element names below are the real ones.

Part oneReading the screen

A navy rail of selects on the left; on the right a long document: an allocation table of up to four portfolio columns, two charts, then a risk dashboard with Nominal/Real pairs. It is the first of two steps; the second attaches products and fees and exports a workbook.

Who

A private wealth advisor (PWA) or their analyst, mid-preparation for a client meeting. Expert in the vocabulary (Core, ex-RAs, CVaR); not an expert in this tool, which they open a few times a month.

The task

Pick the portfolio to propose, then line up to three alternatives beside it so they can say “here is what one notch more risk buys you.” The output is a judgment: which one, and why not the others.

What success looks like

Under two minutes from mandate to a defensible four-column comparison, with the differences already visible — and a clear door to step 2.

The screen is very good at the build half of that job — the guided first build, availability-aware selects, animated row changes and per-column failure are all careful work. It is weaker at the compare half, which is the half the heading promises (“Comparing Portfolio Risk and Return Characteristics”). Almost every recommendation below pushes weight from building to comparing.

Part twoWhere the friction is

  1. The comparison is done in the reader's head. Four columns of absolute weights. To learn that ex-RAs moves 6 points from Other Private Assets into Public Equity you subtract, row by row, across ~25 rows. Nothing on the screen is a difference.
  2. Column identity is lost on scroll. .tbl thead th is position:sticky;top:0, but its scroll container is .tblwrap (overflow-x:auto), which never scrolls vertically — so the header never sticks to the viewport. Halfway down the risk dashboard, “which column is Moderate-Aggressive?” has no answer on screen.
  3. One portfolio, three names. The rail calls it Base portfolio, the table heads it Proposed Portfolio, the scatter legend says Base. And the proposed column hides its own derived name behind a title tooltip, so the one portfolio that matters most is the only one whose identity you can't read.
  4. The key numbers are at the bottom of a table you must scroll. Return, Sharpe and Volatility — the three figures the client conversation is about — sit under ~25 weight rows, and again inside the risk dashboard. There is no at-a-glance read.
  5. No way to change your mind cheaply. If a comparison turns out to be the better proposal, there is no “propose this one instead”; you re-enter it in the rail and re-add the old base through the picker. Columns can't be reordered either.
  6. The way forward is behind you. After the risk dashboard the page simply ends. The only route to step 2 is the step nav at the top of a long document.
  7. The two builders disagree. The rail asks Type → Risk → Allocation → Exclude; the “+” popover asks Allocation → Exclude → Risk. Same decision, different order, and the popover's full-state copy points at a rail list that no longer exists.

A · The comparison itselfMake the screen do the subtraction

A1

Add a “Weights | Difference” switch to the allocation table and risk dashboard

High impactM
Element
#alloc and #risk comparison cells, produced by cellFor(), metricCell() and pairCells().
Change
Put a two-state segmented control in .stage-head, right-aligned where .resolving sits now. In Difference, comparison cells show signed points against the proposed column (+6.0, −2.5, a blank for zero), proposed stays absolute. Colour only beyond ±0.05 so noise stays grey. Persist the choice in the scenario snapshot.
Why
The sentence the PWA needs is “ex-RAs shifts six points of private assets into public equity for 40bp less volatility.” Today that sentence costs ~25 subtractions. In difference mode it is readable in one pass, and blank rows show at a glance where two portfolios are identical — which is most rows.
Tradeoff
I'd make it a mode, not extra columns. A permanent Δ sub-column doubles the width of a table that already needs min-width:850px, and at four portfolios you'd be scrolling sideways on a laptop. The cost of a mode is that a screenshot in Difference can be misread as weights — so tint the header row and suffix comparison heads with “vs proposed” while it's on.

A2

Show the proposed column's real name, and call it one thing everywhere

High impactS
Element
columnHeadCell() / BASE_COLUMN_TITLE; the rail heading in renderBase() (“Base portfolio”); the scatter legend in renderCharts() (“Base”, “Comparison”).
Change
Two-line head for column one: a small PROPOSED chip over the derived name (“Moderate Core”) — the same headerName() string the other columns get. Rename the rail tier to Proposed portfolio and the legend entry to Proposed. Retire “base” from the UI entirely (keep it in code).
Why
The name currently lives only in a title attribute: invisible on touch, slow on hover, absent from a screenshot pasted into an email. And three words for one thing makes a new user wonder whether “base” and “proposed” are different portfolios. The code comment's reasoning for the rename was right; it just wasn't carried to the rail and the chart.
Tradeoff
Two-line heads add ~14px to the header. Worth it: the head is the only place column identity lives.
A3

Give the proposed column a standing visual identity, down its whole height

S
Element
First data column of .tbl.alloc and the first Nominal/Real pair of .tbl.risk.
Change
A 2px accent rule on the column's left edge plus a ~5% accent wash (rgba(31,95,191,.05)), composited under the existing tr.cat/tr.metric fills. Use the same navy #16243A the scatter already uses for the proposed dot as the chip colour so table and chart agree.
Why
All four columns are currently typographically identical. “Proposed vs the rest” is the structure of this screen and it's carried only by position. This also pays for B1: when the header docks, the wash keeps the anchor column findable mid-scroll.
A4

“Propose this instead” on every comparison column

High impactM
Element
.col-head on comparison columns, beside .col-rm.
Change
Replace the lone × with a small kebab (or reveal two 24px glyphs on header hover/focus): Propose this instead and Remove. Promoting swaps column 0 and column i, writes the new key into the rail's selects, and keeps the old proposal as a comparison. Both columns are already resolved, so it needs no fetch — animate it as a swap.
Why
The whole point of comparing is that you might change your mind. Today that costs: re-pick three selects in the rail (which rebuilds), then re-add the old base via the popover. It also matters downstream — step 2 implements column 0, so the wrong portfolio in that slot is an expensive mistake to discover late.
Tradeoff
If sleeves were already chosen in step 2, promotion must run the same revalidateSleeves() path as a base change and say so (“2 sleeves no longer apply”). I'd still ship it; hiding the capability doesn't remove the need.
A5

Reorder the metric rows: Return, Volatility, Sharpe

S
Element
METRIC_ROWS — currently Return, Sharpe, Volatility.
Change
Return → Volatility → Sharpe Ratio, in both tables.
Why
Sharpe is derived from the other two; putting it between them makes the eye jump over the ratio to find the denominator. It also mismatches the scatter card beneath, which is literally titled “Return against volatility”.
Tradeoff
If the legacy PDF report uses today's order and the workbook must match it, keep the workbook and change only the screen — but I'd check whether anyone is attached to that order first.
A6

Distinguish “not held” from “rounds to zero”

S
Element
pct1() (dash under 0.05) and cellFor() (returns '—' when an asset is absent from a held category, '' when the whole category is absent).
Change
Absent → leave blank everywhere. Held but under 0.05% → <0.1% in --ink-3. Reserve the em dash for a failed column only.
Why
One glyph currently means three things: not in this book, present-but-tiny, and the service failed. In an ex-RAs comparison the distinction between “excluded” and “negligible” is the whole story. Blank cells also make difference mode (A1) read cleanly.

B · Layout & hierarchySpend the first screen on the answer

B1

Make the column headers actually stick

DefectHigh impactM
Element
.tbl thead th{position:sticky;top:0} inside .tblwrap{overflow-x:auto}.
Change
The sticky rule is inert: an overflow-x:auto ancestor becomes the sticky containing scrollport, and it never scrolls vertically. Two fixes, pick by width: (a) at ≥1040px, where the tables rarely overflow sideways, set .tblwrap{overflow-x:clip} (or visible) so top:0 resolves against the page; (b) more robust — render one docked header bar, position:fixed under the step nav, that appears once the alloc thead leaves the viewport, mirrors the column names/widths from sizeComparisonTables(), and serves both tables since they share columns.
Why
The risk dashboard is ~30 rows of unlabelled number pairs once its head scrolls off. With four portfolios that's eight anonymous columns. The author clearly intended sticky heads; they just don't engage.
Tradeoff
I'd do (b). It survives the narrow layout where horizontal scroll is unavoidable (sync its scrollLeft with the focused table), and one shared header reinforces that the two tables are the same comparison. It's more code than a one-line CSS change, and it needs aria-hidden so screen readers don't hear the heads twice.
B2

A metric summary strip above the allocation table

High impactM
Element
New, between .stage-head and .tblwrap.accent-top.
Change
One compact card per column, aligned to the table's column grid (reuse the widths sizeFixedColumns() computes): name, then Return · Volatility · Sharpe in --f-num at ~20px, with the delta vs proposed in small type beneath each comparison figure. The cards double as B1's docked header when scrolled.
Why
These three figures decide the conversation and they are currently the last three rows of a 30-row table, then repeated in the dashboard. Leading with them means the first screenful answers “what does one notch of risk buy?” and the weights below become the explanation rather than the preamble.
Tradeoff
It's a third place those numbers appear. I'd resolve that by removing them from the risk dashboard's “Factor Based Risk Analytics” band (see E2), not from the allocation table, whose metric rows mirror the workbook.
B3

Shrink the stage head; drop “Step 1 of 2”

S
Element
.eyebrow “Step 1 of 2”, h2.stage-title at 39px, .stage-sub at 16px.
Change
Remove the eyebrow (the step nav 40px above says the same thing with more authority). Title to ~26px. Fold the sub into the title line in --ink-2, or drop it. Net: ~70px returned to the table.
Why
On a 768px-tall laptop the step nav + eyebrow + 39px title + standfirst consume roughly a quarter of the viewport before any data. This is a working tool opened repeatedly, not a landing page; its title is wayfinding, not a headline. The landing view already does the brand moment.
Tradeoff
The large condensed title is part of the product's look. Keep the face and weight, lose the size.
B4

A “Continue to Implementation” bar at the end of the document

High impactS
Element
New, after the risk dashboard .sec inside #view-aa.
Change
A quiet full-width bar: left, a one-line recap (“Proposing USD Moderate Core · 2 comparisons”); right, a primary button Continue to Implementation → that calls the same handler as [data-step="impl"] (so the D97 greeting still fires). Disabled with a reason while any column is loading.
Why
The page currently just stops. A first-time user who has read to the bottom has to realise the two circles at the very top are the navigation and scroll back to them. Every stepper needs a forward control where the reader finishes, not only where they start.

B5

Move the charts under the summary, or collapse them by default

S
Element
.sec.viz, currently between the two tables.
Change
My preference: keep the scatter, put it beside the B2 strip at wide widths (it is the picture of those same numbers), and demote the stacked bars to a disclosure under the allocation table.
Why

C · The rail & the “+” popoverOne way to describe a portfolio

C1

Same field order in the rail and the popover

S
Element
renderBase(): Type → Risk → Allocation → Exclude. picker.js paint(): Allocation → Exclude → Risk.
Change
Make the popover Risk → Allocation → Exclude, matching the rail and matching headerName()'s output order (“Moderate-Aggressive · Core · ex-RAs”).
Why
The rail's order was reasoned (D54: an all-equity risk level has no allocation, so allocation is a consequence of risk). The popover predates that and wasn't updated — so in the popover, picking Allocation first and then an all-equity risk level silently greys out what you just chose.
C2

Pre-fill the popover with the most likely comparison

S
Element
picker.js cur on open().
Change
Seed it with the proposed key moved one risk level up (if available and unused; else one down). After “Add to table”, advance to the next unused neighbour rather than leaving the just-added, now-disabled combination selected.
Why
The overwhelmingly common comparison is “same book, adjacent risk”. Today that's three interactions per column from a blank form; pre-filled it's one click each, and the popover already stays open for repeat adds — this makes that design pay off.
Tradeoff
A default nudges behaviour. That's fine here: it's a suggestion in an editable form, and adjacent-risk is the honest comparison to show a client.
C3

Fix the stale full-state copy, and the dead list behind it

DefectS
Element
Popover text “All three comparison slots are in use. Remove one from the rail…”, and renderBuilt() writing to #built/#count, which no longer exist in the markup.
Change
Copy → “Remove one with the × on its column.” Delete renderBuilt() or restore the list (see C4). Also don't hardcode “three”: read rules.maxPortfolios − 1.
Why
Instructions that point at a control that isn't there are worse than none.
C4

Show a ghost “add” column instead of a bare “+”, with the slot count

M
Element
th.addcol / #plusbtn.plus and the empty td.addcol cells beneath it.
Change
Render the add column at the same width as a data column, dashed border, centred label “+ Add comparison” with “2 of 3 free” beneath. When only the proposed column exists, give it one line of purpose: “Line up an alternative to show what changes.”
Why
A lone “+” glyph in a header cell is the entry point to the screen's core activity and it's the quietest thing on it. After the carefully guided first build (D91), guidance simply stops — a new user has one column and no hint that comparing is the point. The slot count currently appears only inside the popover.
Tradeoff
Costs a column of width when three real ones are present; at that point collapse it back to the narrow “+”.
C5

Summarise the rail once built, rather than leaving five live selects open

M
Element
#tier-basis and #tier-base; the existing .tier-roll chevron and the Mandate tier's dl.summary + Edit pattern.
Change
After the first successful build, roll Scenario basis and Proposed portfolio up into the same read-only summary the Mandate tier uses (“USD · Hedged”, “PMG Multi-Asset · Moderate · Core”), each with Edit. You already built the roll mechanism for step 2 — use it in step 1 too.
Why
Once the portfolio exists, each of those selects is a one-click destructive rebuild of everything on screen (the basis at least confirms; the base selects don't). Leaving them armed, permanently, at the top of the rail invites accidents and spends the rail's prime space on a finished task. Summary-then-edit also makes the rail consistent across its three tiers.
Tradeoff
One extra click for someone exploring many bases. Mitigate: A4 (promote) and C2 (pre-filled popover) become the fast path for exploration, which is where it belongs.
C6

Say why an option is unavailable

S
Element
Risk options suffixed “— unavailable” in both renderBase() and the popover.
Change
Name the cause you already know: “— not offered in Irish Onshore”, “— needs an allocation with alternatives”. Where the list is long, group with <optgroup label="Not available for this type"> at the bottom so live choices come first.
Why
“Unavailable” reads as an outage. The user can fix it (change type) if told what's gating it.

D · ChartsLet the pictures identify portfolios

D1

Scatter: distinguish the comparisons, and stop labels colliding

M
Element
#viz-rr in renderCharts(): every comparison is the same #1F5FBF dot, labels centred above, truncated at 20 characters.
Change
Number the columns (P, 1, 2, 3), print the number inside each dot, and carry the same numbers into the table heads and the B2 strip; drop the floating text labels or keep them only when there's room. Add a faint connector from the proposed dot to each comparison so direction (“more return, more risk”) is drawn, not inferred.
Why
The most common comparison — Moderate Core vs Moderate Core ex-RAs — produces two dots a few pixels apart with overlapping, truncated labels (“Moderate-Aggressiv…”). Three identical blue dots with a two-entry legend (“Base / Comparison”) can't tell you which is which. Numbered marks are collision-proof and colour-independent.
Tradeoff
Don't assign each portfolio a colour: the seven --cat-* category colours already own colour on this page, and a second categorical palette next to the stacked bars would confuse both.
D2

Link chart and table on hover/focus

M
Element
Stacked segments in #viz-comp, legend items in #viz-key, tr.cat rows in #alloc; dots in #viz-rr and table columns.
Change
Hovering or focusing a category (segment, legend chip or table row) dims the other six in the bars and applies --row-hover to that row. Hovering a dot or a bar applies the A3 wash to that column. Put a small --cat-n swatch in each tr.cat row head so the table is its own legend.
Why
The colour mapping is by name and stable (nice), but the only place it's decoded is a legend above the chart. Seven muted hues in a stack are hard to match by eye; brushing makes the chart an index into the table rather than a separate artefact.
D3

Correct the chart's spoken description

DefectS
Element
aria-label on the bars SVG: “The allocation table below carries the exact values.”
Change
The table is above. Say “the allocation table” and link it (aria-describedby → the table's caption).
Why
A screen-reader user sent the wrong way finds the risk dashboard instead.

E · Risk dashboardThirty rows that need structure

E1

Bind each Nominal/Real pair visually to its portfolio

S
Element
.tbl.risk data columns. Today only the spanning cells of the first band (.span2) carry a left rule; the paired Nominal/Real cells below it carry none.
Change
A 1px --line-strong rule on the left of every portfolio's Nominal column, full height; tighten padding within a pair and widen it between pairs. Print Real in --ink-2 so Nominal leads.
Why
Eight equal-width, equally spaced numeric columns read as eight things. The grouping exists only in a header that (B1) scrolls away. Gestalt proximity does this for free.
E2

Drop the duplicated weights from “Factor Based Risk Analytics”

S
Element
First band of renderRisk(): re-prints every category weight from unionRows() plus all three METRIC_ROWS.
Change
On screen, start the dashboard at “Predicted Performance Over Stress Periods”. Keep the band in the exported workbook, where the sheet must stand alone.
Why
It's ~10 rows identical to numbers one screen up, under a heading (“Factor Based Risk Analytics”) that promises something else. Removing it brings the stress rows — the genuinely new information — a third of a screen closer.
Tradeoff
Breaks strict screen/report parity, which this project values (G6). I'd accept it: the screen is one document, the workbook is separate sheets. If parity wins, at least collapse the band by default.
E3

Make the stress rows scannable: in-cell bars, and mark the worst

M
Element
pairCells(…, signed=true) — red/green text only.
Change
A thin bar behind each stress figure, scaled to the largest absolute value in the band, growing left from the cell's right edge for losses. In each row, bold the best (least negative) portfolio. Add a date range to each crisis label as secondary text if the payload has it.
Why
Nine crises × up to eight columns of two-decimal negatives is a wall; the question is “which of my portfolios holds up, and how much worse is 2008 than 2020?” — a magnitude question that bars answer pre-attentively. Colour alone can't, because nearly every cell is red.
Tradeoff
Bars add ink to a deliberately restrained table. Keep them at ~8% opacity of --neg; they should be felt more than seen.
E4

Let bands fold, and explain the jargon in place

M
Element
tr.band rows (Stress Periods, Value at Risk, Conditional VaR, Probability of Loss).
Change
Turn each band into a disclosure button (you now have the exact pattern in the catalogue's .cat-fold — reuse it, including the keyboard handling). Add a one-line definition as secondary text on the band: “Conditional VaR — average loss in the worst 5% of outcomes.”
Why
PWAs know VaR; the analyst preparing the deck may not know which confidence level this one is, and the client certainly will ask. The definition belongs where the number is, not in a guide.

F · Motion & feedbackThe motion is good; aim it at the right moments

F1

Flash what changed after a rebuild, not just rows that came and went

M
Element
animateRowChanges() / fadeValue().
Change
When a column re-resolves while holdsPreviousData() is true (e.g. hedging toggled, Exclude Real Assets ticked), diff old vs new per cell and give changed figures a 1.2s accent underline that fades. Unchanged cells don't move.
Why
Row entry/exit is handled beautifully, but the far more common event is same rows, different numbers. Toggling hedging changes perhaps six figures out of a hundred, and right now the user has to remember the old ones to notice.
Tradeoff
You've removed prefers-reduced-motion handling (D90). A colour fade isn't motion in the vestibular sense, so this stays within that decision.
F2

Undo on column removal

S
Element
.col-rm → removeComparison().
Change
On remove, show a 6-second toast in the polite live region: “Removed Moderate-Aggressive Core · Undo”. Hold the resolved column in memory so undo is instant; defer deleteColumnOnServer() until the toast expires.
Why
The × is a 24px target sitting 1px from the column name, and it's the only red thing in the header. Mis-clicks cost a popover round-trip and a fetch. Undo is cheaper than a confirm dialog and doesn't slow the deliberate case.
F3

A persistent “saved” signal

S
Element
The spot .resolving occupies in .stage-head; saveSnapshot()/persistBasis().
Change
When idle, show “Saved · 14:02” in --ink-3 there; swap to the spinner while resolving; “Not saved — read only” in the degraded state.
Why
The scenario autosaves, but the user can't know that — there's no Save button and no indication. People about to close a tab before a client meeting need that reassurance; your degraded banner already proves you think about this moment.

G · AccessibilitySpecific gaps in an otherwise careful build

G1

Finish the tab pattern on the step nav — or stop claiming it

DefectS
Element
#steps[role=tablist] with role=tab buttons; #view-aa and #view-impl-wrap have no role=tabpanel, no aria-controls/aria-labelledby, and both tabs stay in the Tab order (no roving tabindex).
Change
Add the panel roles and relationships and roving tabindex (arrow keys are already handled). Alternatively — and I think more honestly — make it <nav> with aria-current="step": these are sequential steps with a gated greeting, not peer tabs.
Why
A half-implemented tablist tells a screen reader “tab 1 of 2” and then offers no panel to move to.
G2

Make the scatter's data reachable without a mouse

S
Element
circle.dot[data-tip] — tooltip on hover only; the SVG is a single role="img".
Change
Give each dot tabindex="0", role="img" and an aria-label (“Moderate Core: volatility 7.03%, return 5.41%”), show the tip on focus, and change the wrapper to role="group". With D1's numbering the label can lead with the number.
Why
Keyboard and touch users currently get no values from the chart at all; the label tells them to go read the table.
G3

Signed figures need more than red and green

S
Element
.tbl td.pos{#1A7F37} / td.neg{#B42318} in the stress band.
Change
Always print the sign — a true minus (U+2212) and an explicit + — via num() when signed. Check the green on the .alt stripe and --row-hover backgrounds; #1A7F37 is ~4.9:1 on white but drops on tinted rows.
Why
Red/green is the commonest colour-vision deficiency, among a user base that skews male. A hyphen-minus in a condensed face at 15px is easy to miss; with the sign explicit the colour becomes reinforcement, which is what it should be. Difference mode (A1) depends on this too.
G4

Announce which column a cell belongs to in the risk table

S
Element
nominalRealStrip() emits <th scope="col">Nominal</th> mid-tbody; portfolio heads use scope="colgroup" but there are no <colgroup> elements per portfolio (one flat colgroup of n×2 cols).
Change
Emit one <colgroup span="2"> per portfolio, give each portfolio th an id, and put headers="p2 nom" on data cells in the paired bands.
Why
As built, a screen reader in a stress cell hears “Nominal, −18.42%” with no portfolio name — scope="colgroup" has no colgroup to resolve against. It's the non-visual version of B1.

DefectS

H3

Narrow tables: fix the label column width and signal the overflow

S
Element
.tbl{min-width:850px} (740px under 900) inside .tblwrap; sticky tbody th with white-space:nowrap.
Change
Under 700px cap the sticky label column at ~44% of the viewport with white-space:normal and a 2-line clamp; add the right-edge scroll shadow you just built for the catalogue (catEdge() generalises directly). Consider a “Proposed + one” mode that shows two columns with a selector for the second.

FinallyWhat to leave alone

Things this screen does better than most tools of its kind — don't let the changes above erode them.

  • The guided first build (card, wire, moving focus). It's the best onboarding moment in the product. C4 extends its spirit; nothing should replace it.
  • Availability-aware selects that disable rather than hide impossible combinations. C6 only asks them to explain themselves.
  • Per-column loading and failure with Retry on the column itself, and old data held while a rebuild resolves. This is why the screen never feels broken.
  • Colour by category name, never position, and colour reserved for categories. D1 deliberately avoids adding a second palette.
  • The basis-change confirm inline in the rail, stating the consequence (“Rebuild 3 portfolios in EUR?”). Right weight, right place.
  • The restrained table typography — condensed numerals, tinted category rows, heavy total. It looks like the document it will become. The job is structure, not decoration.
