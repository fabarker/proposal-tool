Custom sleeves

A sleeve built for one proposal from the products a category already uses, weighted either within the sleeve or straight against the whole portfolio. Admins can always build one; PWAs can where an admin has switched the category on.

Prepared 17 October 2026Status proposal only, no code changedWould be D170Changed since revision 1 the name, who may build, the product list, no reason, and the new weighting switch

In short

Each sleeve drop-down in the rail gains Build a custom sleeve… wherever the person may build one. Admins always can. PWAs can in the categories an admin has switched on in the Repository. The builder offers the products that category's library sleeves already hold. Its weights can be % of the sleeve, which add to 100% and are scaled by the category's strategic weight as today, or % of the portfolio, which sets the category's weight directly. In the second case the other categories scale to make room. Once used, the sleeve is priced, checked, delivered and recorded like a library sleeve, and it belongs to that proposal only.

Who

Admins always. PWAs per category, switched on or off on the Repository's category page.

What

Up to 10 products, from those the category's library sleeves hold under this implementation type.

How much

Weights as % of the sleeve, or % of the portfolio, which overrides the strategic weight.

How it works

Choosing “custom” in the rail

The library sleeves come first as today. Below a divider is Build a custom sleeve…. It appears only when the person may build in that category: always for an admin, and for a PWA when the category is switched on. Overlay-attached categories have no drop-down, so they never get it. Choosing it opens the builder and leaves the current choice in place until the new sleeve is used.

Building the sleeve weighting switch

The builder is a dialog over the page, like the custom fee panel. Its heading gives the category's strategic weight and dollars. Every row shows what its weight buys in dollars, against the product's minimum.

  • Start from: blank, or a copy of a library sleeve offered here, whose products and weights are then changed.
  • Name: optional, 80 characters at most. If left empty the sleeve is called “Custom”. Note: optional.
  • Weights are: % of the sleeve or % of the portfolio. This is your new point, described in full in its own section. Switching converts the numbers, so the sleeve means the same until you change one.
  • Spread evenly shares the total across the rows: 100% of the sleeve, or the category's strategic weight of the portfolio.

The mock-up below works. Switch the weighting, change a weight or remove a row. In % of the portfolio, “Put the example back” sets the sleeve to 20% of the portfolio, and the panel shows the other categories making room.

The checks, live and again on the server
  • Products are in the category's list, live in the catalogue, each once, each weight above zero. There are no more than 10.
  • In % of the sleeve: the weights add to exactly 100%.
  • In % of the portfolio: the total is above zero, and leaves room for the categories that make room (below).
  • No product falls below its minimum investment at this mandate. This is the export's own rule, shown while building rather than at the end.

Adding products

+ Add products opens a picker inside the dialog. It is the Repository's “Add a product” dialog, moved to code both screens share: search, filters for vehicle, style and source, sortable cost and minimum columns, tick several and add them together. It lists only the category's products: those its library sleeves hold under this implementation type, including disabled sleeves, but not archived ones. To make another product available in a category, an admin puts it in one of that category's library sleeves.

Back on the page

After Use this sleeve, the drop-down reads Custom: IG with sterling credit, with the product count, cost and Edit under it. In the table the category's band carries a violet Custom pill. When the sleeve is weighted as % of the portfolio, the band also shows the strategic weight it replaced, struck through. The other categories show their new weights, and each one that moved shows its strategic weight struck through beside it.

Changing it, or going back to the library

  • Edit reopens the builder on the sleeve as it stands. Cancel leaves it unchanged.
  • Picking a library sleeve switches back to it. The custom sleeve waits in the drop-down as Custom: IG with sterling credit and comes back unchanged if picked again. One choice is in force at a time. If the custom sleeve set the category's weight, going back to the library also puts the strategic weights back.
  • Discard the custom sleeve, at the foot of the builder, removes it from the proposal.
  • A change of implementation type clears it with every other sleeve choice (your answer 9).
  • A change of base portfolio or currency keeps it. In % of the sleeve it follows the new strategic weight. In % of the portfolio it keeps its weight and the others make room again. The product list is checked again, and its minimums re-run.

Where the difference goes

If Investment Grade Fixed Income is set to 20% where the strategic allocation says 24%, 4% has to go somewhere. The proposal is that it goes to the other categories in proportion, the same way an overlay takes its weight from its source categories today. Each category keeps its share of the remainder, and so does every product inside it. Two kinds of category do not move: those an overlay fills, which keep their overlay size, and other custom sleeves set as % of the portfolio, which keep what was typed.

scale = (100% − overlay categories − every % of portfolio custom sleeve) ÷ (what the remaining categories add to after the overlays) a remaining category's weight × scale; every product in it follows

In the example: 100% − 20% = 80% is shared by categories that held 76%, so each scales by 80/76 = 1.0526. Public Equity goes from 46.00% to 48.42%, and the three 10% categories go to 10.53%, 10.53% and 10.52%. The rounding is the workbook's own largest-remainder rounding, so the total is exactly 100.00%.

The order things happen in

  1. The strategic allocation, from the base portfolio.
  2. The overlays, exactly as today: they take from their sources and fill their own categories.
  3. Custom sleeves weighted as % of the portfolio take their typed weight. Every other category scales to fill what is left.
  4. Library sleeves and custom sleeves weighted as % of the sleeve spread each category's weight across their products. Then rounding, minimums, fees, the private funding split and the export checks run, all as today.

Because step 3 comes after the overlays, a % of the portfolio custom sleeve gets exactly what was typed even when an overlay draws on that category. For example, if the tactical tilt takes from Investment Grade Fixed Income and the custom sleeve says 20%, the result is 20%, and the tilt's take falls on the categories that scale.

When it is refused

  • The typed total is zero, or leaves nothing for the categories that make room (for example 100% with other categories still holding weight).
  • A product would fall below its minimum. The builder checks the sleeve's own products. Products in the categories that shrink are checked live in the rail and the table, and at export, as today.
What the risk and return figures describe

The Portfolios and Risk sheets, and the deck's analytics, are worked out from the strategic allocation. They do not use the implementation. That is already the case with overlays, which move the implemented weights away from the strategic ones. A % of the portfolio custom sleeve can move them further, by as much as the person types. The figures stay as they are: they describe the strategic allocation. Question C asks whether the delivered files should say that the implementation departs from it.

Switching it on for PWAs

Each category's page in the Repository's Sleeves tab, the screen you showed me with the Disable buttons, gets a switch in its header: Custom sleeves for PWAs. It starts off for every category. A category switched off still lets admins build. The switch applies under every implementation type, as you asked. The per-type product lists keep an ESG proposal to the products the ESG library already uses.

  • The setting is kept as a new kind in the existing policies table, with a history. Each switch is recorded with who and when, and appears in the Activity tab under Custom sleeves. No sleeves schema change is needed.
  • Turning a category off stops PWAs building or changing custom sleeves there. A proposal that already holds one keeps it, so a PWA can still deliver it. They see it but cannot change it, and the drop-down offers to switch back to a library sleeve.
  • A custom sleeve an admin built in a switched-off category is handled the same way: the PWA can deliver it but not change it.
  • Make it a library sleeve: from the register, an admin opens the New sleeve form filled with a custom sleeve's products. For a % of the portfolio sleeve, the weights are converted to % of the sleeve. The admin names it and creates it through the normal route.

What the client sees

As you decided, nothing marks a sleeve as custom in the workbook or the deck. The implementation pages show the weights as implemented, so a % of the portfolio sleeve shows there at its typed weight, with the other categories at their scaled weights, exactly as an overlay's effect already shows. The asset allocation, portfolio and risk pages keep showing the strategic allocation.

What gets recorded

  • The register: each custom row in proposalSleeves has no library id or version, and gains kind (library or custom), mode (sleeve or portfolio), products (id and weight each) and note. The proposal also records each category's strategic and implemented weight, so a departure from the strategic allocation can be seen later. Register schema 7: added columns only.
  • The register's screens: a Custom chip, a Custom saved view, and the composition and weighting in the detail panel. A proposal whose implemented weights depart from the strategic ones because of a custom sleeve says so there.
  • The CSV: Investment Grade Fixed Income=Custom: IG with sterling credit (% of portfolio, 20.00%), with the composition in its own column.
  • Account opening requests echo the register's pins, marked custom.

Cases worth thinking through

  • A product leaves the category's list after the sleeve is built (an admin removed it from the library sleeves, or withdrew it). The builder flags it and the export refuses with the product's name. The PWA swaps it out.
  • Two custom sleeves, both % of the portfolio, are both fixed. Together they must leave room for the categories that scale.
  • Every non-overlay category custom and % of the portfolio: nothing is left to scale, so the typed totals plus the overlays must add to exactly 100%. The builder says so.
  • The mandate is cut after building: minimums are re-run in the rail at once, as now.
  • Base or currency change: % of the sleeve follows the new strategic weight; % of the portfolio keeps its own and the others re-scale.
  • Two people on one proposal: it saves with the rest of the proposal, last save wins, as for other choices.

Moving Poros across

All four stages go as one porting/update.py, the usual single copied file. On Poros it adds one route for PWAs, two admin routes and register schema 7 (columns added on first open, so existing proposals are kept), and seeds the custom sleeve settings with every category off. Poros's sleeves database needs no change. Nothing changes for PWAs on Poros until an admin switches a category on.

How it gets tested

  • Same answer both ways. A proposal built from a library sleeve and from a custom sleeve with the same products and weights, in % of the sleeve, produces identical rows, fees, minimums, workbook cells and deck tables.
  • The two weightings agree when they mean the same thing: % of the portfolio weights that add to the strategic weight give exactly the % of the sleeve result, and nothing else moves.
  • The scaling: the worked example above to the cent; overlays first; two fixed sleeves; refusal when nothing is left; largest-remainder rounding to 100.00%; private funding split after scaling.
  • Page and server agree on step 3 and on the rows, run through node as the existing mirror tests are.
  • Who may build: admin always; PWA on and off; an existing sleeve kept but frozen when switched off.
  • Register schema 6 to 7 in place; the CSV and the view counts.
  • Clicked through on the sandbox with screenshots before anything reaches your service.
