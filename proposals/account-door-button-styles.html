Landing page · the second door

Ten doors

Ten treatments for Open an Account, each drawn at real size beside the real Start Here, on the page’s own ground. Pick by number.

How to read these. Every strip below is the landing page’s lower half at 1 : 1 — same 50px height, same padding, same square corners, same #FBFCFE ground. The door is pushed to the right edge exactly as it is on the page.

On a desktop you can hover each one to see its pressed-state colour; on a phone the Hover line under each card says what it does.

Where we areAs built today

NOW

White, hairline border

Current

A plain surface with the page’s standard #D4DAE2 hairline. It is correct but anonymous — at a glance it reads as a disabled control or a form field rather than a way in. That is what we are fixing.

Hover border and text go accent blue.

The tenSuggestions

Roughly ordered from most conservative to most distinctive. Three are solid, four are outlined, three are minimal.

01

Navy outline

Recommended

Borrows the page’s own darkest ink — the 2px rule above the eyebrow and the title itself are both this navy — so it introduces no new colour at all. A 1.5px edge instead of 1px gives it enough weight to read as deliberate rather than defaulted.

Hover fills solid navy, text goes white.

.lp-second{background:var(--surface);color:#16243A; border:1.5px solid #16243A} .lp-second:hover{background:#16243A;color:#fff; border-color:#16243A}
02

Navy solid

Heavy

Equal visual weight to Start Here but a different colour, so the two read as a genuine pair of choices rather than an action and its shadow. The cost is real: two filled buttons at opposite ends of the column compete, and the blue one stops being obviously first.

Hover lifts one step to #22344F.

.lp-second{background:#16243A;color:#fff; border-color:#16243A} .lp-second:hover{background:#22344F; border-color:#22344F}
03

Accent outline

Classic secondary

The textbook pairing: same colour family as the primary, hollow instead of filled. Unambiguous hierarchy, and the two obviously belong together. The catch is that belonging together is slightly the wrong message — these are different jobs, not two grades of the same one.

Hover fills accent blue, text goes white.

.lp-second{background:var(--surface);color:var(--accent); border:1.5px solid var(--accent)} .lp-second:hover{background:var(--accent);color:#fff}
04

Accent tint, no border

Soft

A pale blue surface instead of an outline. Nothing on the page goes grey and hairline-thin, which is the failure mode of the current treatment on a near-white ground. Reads as a surface you press rather than a box you enter.

Hover deepens to #DCE7F8.

.lp-second{background:#E9F0FB;color:#1A4FA0; border-color:#E9F0FB} .lp-second:hover{background:#DCE7F8;color:#164486}
05

Graphite solid

HeavyNeutral

Solid weight without navy’s formality. It steps out of the blue family entirely, so the accent stays unambiguously the primary’s property. Quieter than 02 while still being a filled button.

Hover darkens to #3B4959.

.lp-second{background:#4A5A6B;color:#fff; border-color:#4A5A6B} .lp-second:hover{background:#3B4959;border-color:#3B4959}
06

Underline only

Minimal

No box at all — the label with a 2px accent rule beneath it. The lower half drops to one button and one line of text, which is the quietest this can get while still pointing at the right edge. The risk is real: with nothing enclosing it and 700px of space to its left, it may not read as pressable.

Hover text goes accent blue, underline stays.

.lp-second{background:none;border-color:transparent; color:#16243A;padding-inline:4px; text-decoration:underline; text-decoration-color:var(--accent); text-decoration-thickness:2px; text-underline-offset:6px}
07

Accent left rule

Minimal

A box that is only a box on one side. The 3px bar echoes the square accent markers on the two bullet points directly above it and the colour bar above those — the page already speaks in short rectilinear marks, and this is one more.

Hover faint blue wash behind, text goes accent.

.lp-second{background:var(--surface); border-color:transparent;color:#16243A; border-left:3px solid var(--accent);padding-left:22px} .lp-second:hover{background:#F4F8FE;color:var(--accent)}
08

Spectrum top edge

Distinctive

A 4px strip across the top in all seven category colours, tying the button to the colour bar directly above it — the one piece of identity the page owns. Unmistakably this product and nothing else. It is also the only option here that could date badly, or read as decorative on a button that is not about categories.

Hover hairline darkens to #8494A4; the strip holds.

.lp-second{background:var(--surface);color:#16243A; border-color:var(--line-strong); padding-top:15px;position:relative} .lp-second::before{content:"";position:absolute; left:-1px;right:-1px;top:-1px;height:4px; background:linear-gradient(to right, var(--cat-1) 0 14.28%, ... var(--cat-7) 85.71% 100%)}
09

Gold outline

DistinctiveNew colour

A warm counterpoint that says different track without raising its voice. The tool has no gold anywhere else, which is both the appeal and the cost — it introduces a colour the design system would then have to account for.

Hover fills gold, text goes white.

.lp-second{background:var(--surface);color:#8A6520; border:1.5px solid #B7862A} .lp-second:hover{background:#B7862A;color:#fff}
10

Ghost, framed on hover

Minimal

At rest it is only a label in secondary ink at the right edge; it becomes a button the moment you reach for it. The lightest possible footprint, and it keeps the lower half visually down to one object. Same discoverability risk as 06, slightly worse — there is not even an underline to mark it as live.

Hover white fill and the standard hairline appear.

.lp-second{background:none;color:var(--ink-2); border-color:transparent} .lp-second:hover{background:var(--surface); color:#16243A;border-color:var(--line-strong)}

If you want oneWhat I’d pick

01, navy outline. It is the only option that adds weight without adding a colour: navy is already the page’s rule and its title, so the lower half gains definition and the palette stays at two. It also keeps the hierarchy honest — blue means go and belongs to Start Here, while navy means this is the other thing.

If you want the page to have a signature rather than just be correct, 08 is the one worth the argument. If you think the second door is currently too loud for how rarely it is used, 07 is the honest way down — not 10, which disappears entirely on a touch screen.

Worth decidingTwo things these don’t settle

  • The label. Every mock-up says Open an Account. Since the caption came off, nothing on the landing page says this needs a delivered proposal. Open an Account is the shortest honest label; Account Opening Request is the accurate one and about 40% wider.
  • Whether it should pulse. Start Here carries a slow shadow pulse and an expanding halo. Nothing here borrows it, on purpose — two pulsing buttons is one too many — but a single quiet lift on hover would be cheap to add to whichever you pick.
