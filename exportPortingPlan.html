Proposal Tool · porting plan

Cutting epsilonPhi out of the Excel export

A plan to remove Reporting and SAAPortfolio from workbook generation so the service can be ported into cyrus, while the output stays exactly what it is today. Written after tracing the export end to end and comparing both writers cell by cell against a real two-column proposal.

Written 3 September 2026 Companion to exportTrace.html Verdict feasible, and smaller than it looks Status built (D67) — zero cell differences, assumptions kept as a fourth sheet

Outcome

Built, and the comparison comes out at zero. All three engine-laid sheets are reproduced cell for cell — values, number formats, fonts, fills, borders, alignment, all thirty merges, row heights, column widths and the conditional rule. The assumptions sheet is no longer discarded: it is the fourth sheet, fed by per-asset estimates the bake now records once per slice.

An export takes ~0.13 s where it took 23.9 s warm and 75 s cold, and imports no part of the analytics library. Phase 0's golden workbook is committed under tests/golden/ with the comparison wired into the suite, so this cannot drift.

1The finding that decides this

Before proposing anything I generated the same export twice — once through the engine, once through the engine-free writer — and compared every cell of the two engine-built sheets against the numbers already sitting in the baked payloads.

90 / 90risk_dashboard numbers already in the payload, exact to full double precision
48 / 48portfolios weight cells, likewise
0numbers the engine puts in the workbook that the service does not already hold
0charts, images, pivot tables or formulas anywhere in the workbook

The whole plan rests on this

Reporting contributes no information — only layout. Every figure on both engine-built sheets is already carried by the baked payloads: category and asset weights, the three metrics, nine stress periods with nominal and real, and nine risk-premia rows across VaR, CVaR and probability of loss. The workbook is pure cells and styles; there is nothing in it that needs a live analytics object to exist.

So this is not "reimplement the analytics". It is "write 72 known numbers into a known layout" — and the service already writes a very similar layout in 63 milliseconds.

Two supporting observations from the run:

  • The engine does far more work than the workbook shows. The log records factor stress losses computed for reference portfolios at 0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7 and 0.8 equity — none of which reach either sheet. Roughly 97 seconds of computation for two columns, and the sheets keep 90 numbers of it.
  • The bake already covers everything. 43 portfolios per currency × 4 currencies × 4 hedging policies = 688 payloads, no gaps. Every portfolio a user can select already has its analytics on disk.

The two conversions that will bite

The sign flip. liveAdapter stores VaR and CVaR already negated (sign = -1.0) because the screen shows them as losses. The sheet shows them positive. Anything reading premia for the workbook must flip them back — and only for kind == 'loss', never for the probability rows.

The scale asymmetry. On the portfolios sheet, category rows are fractions with 0.0% and asset rows are ×100 with 0.0. That is the house report's own convention, already preserved deliberately in workbook.py, and it must survive.

4The plan

Phase 0

Capture golden files while epsilonPhi is still reachable

~half a day · do this first

This is the phase that makes the rest safe, and the only one with a deadline. Once the package is in cyrus, the reference output can never be regenerated. Capture it now, commit it, and every later change is checkable against it.

  1. Pick a matrix of scenarios that exercises the shape of the workbook, not the space of the analytics: 1 / 2 / 4 columns; fees on and off; tactical tilt on and off; volatility premium on and off; a currency with a substitution (GBP) and one without (USD); an all-equity book (no allocation type) and a book with an excluded category. Roughly 16 cases.
  2. Generate each through LiveScenarioPort.build_export and commit the .xlsx files alongside the resolved payloads that produced them.
  3. Write the comparator: opens two workbooks and asserts every cell's value, number format, font, fill, border, merge, row height, column width and conditional-formatting rule. Report differences as a list, not a boolean.
Gate: the comparator passes when a golden file is compared with itself, and reports a precise diff when compared with today's writer output — the eleven differences in section 3 should be exactly what it prints.
Phase 1

Bring the writer to parity

~2–3 days

Work the comparator's diff list down to zero. Split writeFixturesWorkbook into three functions with one job each — writePortfoliosSheet, writeRiskDashboardSheet, and the existing writeImplementationSheet — so the two reproduced sheets can be tested in isolation.

Nothing about the analytics changes. The input is the same list of resolved payloads the export already passes in.

Gate: all 16 golden cases match cell-for-cell and style-for-style. Add the comparison to the test suite so a future edit to the stylesheet cannot silently move the workbook.
Phase 2

Make the writer the only export path

~half a day

Once parity is proved, the fork disappears:

  • BakedScenarioPort.build_export stops delegating and always calls the writer.
  • LiveScenarioPort.build_export is deleted, taking with it the Reporting import, the deepcopy, the temp directory and the second workbook serialisation.
  • Reporting comes out of _SYMBOLS in engine.py.

Immediate effect, before any porting: the export drops from ~24 seconds to ~60 milliseconds, and the cold-start 75-second case disappears entirely.

Gate: the export produces byte-comparable output with SCENARIO_BAKED_FALLBACK=0 and no epsilonPhi module in sys.modules — assert that in the test.
Phase 3

Close the remaining runtime paths to the engine

~1–2 days

The export is only one of two doors. resolve_portfolio is the other, and the baked adapter still falls through to the live one on a miss. For a cyrus deployment that fallback cannot exist, so it must become impossible rather than merely switched off:

  • Add an explicit engine-free mode — a single setting under which registry.py never constructs a LiveScenarioPort at all, so nothing can lazily resolve an engine symbol.
  • Make an unbaked key a clear, actionable error rather than a silent fallback. It already is when the fallback is off; keep that message and make sure the UI shows it well.
  • Move bake.py, liveAdapter.py, engine.py and the engine-touching parts of portfolio_weights.py behind a boundary that the cyrus port simply does not carry — a build-time package, not a runtime one.
Gate: a test that imports the whole service in engine-free mode, exercises schema, resolve, sleeves and export, and asserts no module beginning epsilonPhi was ever imported.

5The honest limit of this

The dependency moves; it does not vanish

Removing Reporting and SAAPortfolio from the service is achievable and the output can be identical. But the numbers in the baked store were computed by epsilonPhi, and refreshing them still needs it.

So the shape after this work is: cyrus runs a service with no analytics library at all, serving a baked store; and the bake is produced here, on a machine that has epsilonPhi, and shipped to cyrus as data. The dependency moves from runtime to build time — which is exactly what makes the port possible, but it is a supply chain, not an elimination.

Two consequences worth deciding on now:

Consequence 1

The store becomes a delivery

The baked directory stops being a cache and becomes a versioned artefact with an owner, a cadence and a hand-off — the same treatment the fee card gets. The manifest already records what it was built from, so most of the machinery exists.

Consequence 2

The service can no longer compute anything new

A portfolio outside the bake cannot be answered at all. Today that is fine — coverage is complete, all 688. It stops being fine the moment the SAA extract gains a portfolio and the bake has not been re-run, so the staleness check already recommended (G1) stops being a nicety.

8What I would want from you before starting

Decision 1

Is "exactly" exact?

The plan assumes cell-for-cell and style-for-style identity, including reproducing the empty Portfolio Risk Premia header and the lowercase sheet names. If some of that is incidental rather than required, the comparator can be relaxed and Phase 1 gets shorter.

Decision 2

Who owns the bake after the port?

It becomes a delivery into cyrus. Someone has to produce it, version it and hand it over on a cadence — and that is a different job from running the service.

Decision 3

Does the live adapter survive at all?

It could stay here as the bake's engine and never ship to cyrus, or be retired entirely in favour of the bake being the only way analytics ever enter. The first is less disruptive; the second is a cleaner boundary.

Evidence gathered by building the same two-column export (USD Moderate Full and USD Conservative Full, $50m, five sleeves, fees on) through both paths and comparing them programmatically: 90 of 90 risk-dashboard cells and 48 of 48 portfolio weight cells matched the baked payloads exactly; the workbook was confirmed to contain no charts, images, pivot tables or formulas; and the eleven presentation differences in section 3 were enumerated from the two files directly.
