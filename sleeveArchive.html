Proposal Tool · sleeve repository · design exploration

Viewing archived sleeves

A minimal version of this shipped with the history work: a Removed entry in the category rail listing what has been taken out of the library. This is about what it should actually be — five ways to build the archive, what each one is good at, and the four decisions that pick between them.

What exists today, honestly

Worth being precise about the baseline, because two of the five options below are extensions of it rather than replacements.

Shipped · the Removed view

A Removed entry appears at the foot of the category rail when anything has been taken out. It lists every removed sleeve across all four books — name, category, implementation type, who removed it and when, how many versions are kept — and opening one shows what it held when it went, with Put back in the library.

It is a flat, unsorted list with no search. Fine for the one or two removals a quarter that the library sees today. Useless at fifty.

Shipped · the History drawer

Under the editor for a live sleeve: every revision newest first, what moved in each, the whole composition one click further, and Put this version back on anything not in force.

It is per-sleeve and closed by default. You have to already know which sleeve you are asking about, and be looking at it.

The four questions neither can answer

"What changed in the library last month?"noHistory is per-sleeve. There is no view that crosses sleeves, so answering it means opening 101 drawers.
"Show me the library as it stood when this proposal was written."noNothing reads the record at a date. See the note below — this one matters more than it looks.
"Put back the six sleeves we retired in the ESG rebuild."one at a timeRestore is per-sleeve, and there is no way to select the six as a group.

The thing that makes this more than housekeeping

A proposal pins a sleeve's name, not its version. The scenario store keeps {category: sleeve name} — so a proposal written in June against SMA Only at 45/35/20 will, reopened today, show whatever SMA Only holds now. The numbers on the page will have moved and nothing will say so.

The record to answer "what did this proposal actually propose?" now exists — it is in sleeveHistory. Nothing reads it that way yet. Whether the archive is built to answer that question is the single biggest decision here, because it is the difference between a convenience for admins and a control the business relies on.

The questions an archive gets asked

Every option below is judged against these. They are not equally important, and they do not all pull in the same direction — which is why there is more than one sensible answer.

1
Find one thing I half-remember.

A name, or part of one. The commonest use by far, and the one the current view is worst at.

2
What happened to this sleeve?

Per-sleeve history. Already answered well by the drawer.

3
What happened to the library?

Across all sleeves, over a period. Nothing answers this today.

4
What did we offer on a given date?

The point-in-time question. Needed to read an old proposal honestly, and the one an auditor asks.

5
Undo something.

One sleeve, or a batch. Per-sleeve is solved; batches are not.

6
Prove it to somebody else.

An export of the record, not a screen. Compliance, an audit, a handover.

The decisions that shape it

Decision 1

Place, state, or feed?

Is the archive somewhere you go (a tab), something the library can be shown as (a toggle), or a stream of what happened? Each is a different mental model and they do not combine for free.

Decision 2

Sleeve or change?

Is a row in the archive a sleeve that is gone, or an edit that happened? Question 1 wants sleeves. Question 3 wants changes. This is the fork.

Decision 3

Removed only, or superseded too?

Today "archived" means removed. But a sleeve edited nine times has eight archived versions that are just as gone. Including them multiplies the archive by roughly ten.

Decision 4

What is it called?

The code says deleted, the console says removed, you said archived. Three words for one thing is how a feature gets explained twice. Pick one now.

Option A · Archive as a third tab

Option A · recommended, with C

A place you go, with a proper search over it

The archive leaves the category rail and becomes a peer of Sleeves and Catalogue. One searchable, sortable table of everything removed, filtered by book, category, who and when. Selecting a row shows what it held, its whole history, and restores it.

Answers

  • 1 — search, properly
  • 5 — multi-select restores a batch
  • 6 — the table exports as it stands
  • 2 — the drawer comes with it

Does not answer

  • 3 — still sleeve-shaped, not change-shaped
  • 4 — no notion of a date to stand at

Cost

Moderate. Reuses the catalogue's table, facets and search wholesale.

Option B · A toggle over the library

Option B

The archive as a state of the thing you are already looking at

No new place. A Show archived switch folds removed sleeves back into their own category, greyed and badged, in their real position. The category counts show both numbers. You browse the library as it is, or as it has ever been, without going anywhere.

Answers

  • 1 — if you know the category
  • 5 — restore in place, in context
  • Strongest at "what else was in this category?"

Does not answer

  • 3, 4, 6 — none of them
  • 1 when you have forgotten the category

Cost

Small. One flag, one filter, some styling. The cheapest thing here by a distance.

Option C · The activity timeline

Option C · recommended, with A

The unit is the change, not the sleeve

One reverse-chronological feed of every revision across the whole library, grouped by day, filterable by book, category, person and action. Each row says what happened in a sentence and opens into the version it produced. This is the record read as a story rather than as a filing cabinet.

Answers

  • 3 — the only option that does
  • 6 — an audit trail, exportable
  • 5 — "undo everything from Tuesday" becomes selectable

Does not answer

  • 1 — finding one sleeve in a feed is the wrong shape
  • 4 — adjacent to it, but not it

Cost

Moderate. One new query over sleeveHistory; the data is already there and indexed.

Why this earns its place beside A rather than instead of it. A filing cabinet and a story answer different questions and neither substitutes for the other. The feed is what you open on a Monday to see what moved; the archive table is what you open when you are hunting one thing. The feed is also the honest home of the governance case — it is the view that reads as an audit trail, and the operations framework's weekly sleeve check becomes a thirty-second scroll rather than a CLI census.

Option D · The library as at a date

Option D

Stand at a moment and look at the whole library

A date control over the Sleeves view. Move it and the entire console reconstructs the library as it stood then — every sleeve that existed, holding what it held. Read-only in the past, with a way to bring one forward. Ticks on the track mark days something changed.

Answers

  • 4 — the only option that does
  • 6 — the strongest possible proof
  • Reads an old proposal honestly

Does not answer

  • 1, 3 — not what it is for
  • 5 — bringing a whole day forward is a bigger idea than it looks

Cost

Large. Every read in the repository needs an "as at" parameter. The most work here by some way.

Option E · Just fix what is there

Option E

Keep the rail entry; give it a search box and a sort

The Removed view stays where it is and gains the three things it lacks: a search over name, category and product; a sort by date or name; and a date-range chip. Nothing moves, nothing new is learned.

Answers

  • 1 — adequately
  • Nothing else changes

Does not answer

  • 3, 4, 5, 6

Cost

An afternoon.

The case for it. The library has 101 sleeves and nothing archived. Every option above is sized for a problem that does not exist yet, and this one buys eighteen months of headroom for an afternoon's work. The case against: the rail entry is in the wrong place conceptually — an archive is not a category — so this is money spent on a shape that will be thrown away. Worth taking only as a deliberate holding position while A and C are decided.

What I would build

Recommendation

A and C together, as one piece of work, with D held as a second phase.

They cost about the same as each other and they cover five of the six questions between them, because they answer along different axes — A files sleeves, C narrates changes. Building only one leaves an obvious hole: A alone still cannot say what happened last week, and C alone makes finding one old gilts sleeve a scroll through 412 events. Both reuse machinery the catalogue view already has, so the marginal cost of the second is much less than the first.

D is the one with the real business case — the proposal question and the audit question are the same question — but it is also the one that touches every read in the repository. It deserves its own decision rather than being smuggled in, and it lands better once A and C exist to click through to.

B is what I would not build. It is the cheapest and the most immediately pleasant, and it degrades fastest: it is the only option that gets actively worse as the archive grows, which is the one thing an archive is guaranteed to do.

A sensible sequence

First

The Archive tab and the Activity feed

Retire the rail entry. One new endpoint each over sleeveHistory, both views built on the catalogue's table and facet machinery. Batch restore and CSV export fall out of the table almost free.

Then

Settle the vocabulary and the scope

One word everywhere, and a decision on whether superseded versions appear in the Archive alongside removed sleeves, or stay only in Activity and the drawer.

Later, deliberately

As at a date

Only if the proposal-reading or audit case is real. It is a phase of its own, and it is the one that makes the record load-bearing rather than merely available.

What I would need from you

1
Is the point-in-time question real?

Does anyone need to read an old proposal against the library it was written against, or show an auditor what was offered on a date? A yes makes D worth planning for now, and changes how A and C are built. A no takes the largest piece of work off the table entirely.

2
Archived, removed, or retired?

One word, used in the code, the console and the CLI. I would take archived, since that is the word you reached for.

3
Does the Archive hold superseded versions too, or only removed sleeves?

Today's answer is removed sleeves only. Including versions makes it roughly ten times larger and much more useful for question 3 — but Activity may be the better home for those.

4
Who sees it?

Everything here is behind requireAdmin, like the rest of the repository. Should a PWA be able to see that a sleeve their proposal names has changed since? That is a different feature, and worth knowing now if the answer is yes.

5
Does anything ever leave the record?

The history has no pruning and none is proposed — a few hundred bytes per revision makes this a decade-scale question. But if there is a retention policy the record has to obey, it needs saying before the archive is built on the assumption of permanence.

Grounded in the shipped D65 record: sleeveHistory holding one append-only row per revision, soft-deleted sleeves, and sleeveTools --history / --deleted. The library today holds 101 sleeves, 101 revisions and nothing archived — every option is drawn against the library it will be at 400 revisions, not the one it is at 101.
